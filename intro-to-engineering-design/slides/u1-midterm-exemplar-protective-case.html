This is the exemplar for the Dec 16 midterm presentation. It follows the 11-slide outline in the project packet, in checklist order, with a few extra slides where a strong team would add evidence. Orange "coach" boxes say which rubric criterion a slide earns and why it reads as a 4. Press C to hide them, which shows students what the actual presentation looks like. The device is a made-up "Demo Phone" (150 × 72 × 8 mm) so nobody can copy this case onto their own phone. The model is real and printable. 3 · Develop the Solution · 3D model

Two components, one per partner

Exploded assembly yours: Fusion screenshots

Shell (Partner A): back, side walls and lip around the bottom 104 mm of the phone, plus the USB-C and speaker slots and both button slots. It ends in a 6 mm tongue with two detent bumps.

Snap cap (Partner B): the top 48 mm with the camera notch. A 6 mm socket slides over the tongue, and the bumps click into Ø2.2 holes.

Both parts share one parameter table, so changing the clearance changes both.

Rubric 3 · CADSays who modeled what, names the joint, and shows the parts are driven by parameters rather than typed-in numbers.

3 · Develop the Solution · Assembly

How it goes together

  1. Phone slides down into the shell from the open top end. The side lips and bottom wall hold it.
  2. Cap slides down over the shell's tongue (0.2 mm clearance per side).
  3. Click. Two 0.4 mm detent bumps snap into the cap's holes. Squeeze the cap sides to release it.

Cap, back view: the camera notch is open at the bottom edge

Rubric 3 + 7 · CommunicationThree numbered steps and one picture explain the mechanism faster than a paragraph would.

These are the actual problems found while building this exemplar. Fusion's Interference tool (Inspect → Interference) does the same check. Why D < C: inside edges print about 0.2 mm small because the extruded line squishes inward. The 0.4 clearance absorbs that, leaving about 0.2 per side, so the phone is snug without rattling.

Rubric 4 · TolerancesAll four columns, plus the error, plus a physical reason for the error. The section shows exactly where the clearance is.
Column D here is illustrative: it's the typical direction and size of FDM error on inside features (undersize by 0.1–0.3 mm, worse across XY than in Z). Students must fill D from their own calipers. If a team's D column equals C exactly, they didn't measure. The socket row is the joint: designed 0.2 per side, which leaves about 0.1 after printing, a snug slip fit.

5 · Evaluate · Next iteration & reflection

Version 4 and what we learned

Fix criterion 8: raise the detents from 0.4 to 0.6 mm and add a second pair. Re-test 10 drops with the dummy.

Add margin on criterion 3: make the floor 2.4 mm so the back stands 0.9 mm above the camera, not 0.5.

Thin socket wall (0.8 mm): it's two perimeters. Add a 0.5 mm chamfer at the tongue's entry so the cap starts straight and doesn't crack the wall.

What we learned: the phone didn't fail at its final position. It failed on the way in. Checking the insertion path in CAD saved us two reprints.

Feedback we used: a classmate at design review asked "how does the cap get past the camera?", which led to the notch.

Rubric 6 + 1 · ReflectionThere's a specific, testable fix for each weakness, and the team names the feedback that changed the design.
