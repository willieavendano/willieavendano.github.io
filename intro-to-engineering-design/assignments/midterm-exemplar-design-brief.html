Project 1.3.7 Design a Protective Case: Phone Edition · the Slide & Snap Case · Exemplar Team

How to read this pageThis is the packet's Design Brief, filled in by the exemplar team for a made-up “Demo Phone.” Printed text is the brief as given. Blue text is what the team wrote, including the blanks they filled in. Their brief is the first two slides of the exemplar presentation. Use it for format; your device, numbers and criteria must be your own.
ClientThe phone's owner: you or your partner. → Partner A, owner of the Demo Phone.
Target consumerStudents who carry a phone in a pocket, backpack or bag every day.
Designer(s)Exemplar Team: Partner A (case shell, Part A) and Partner B (snap cap, Part B).
DeviceMake / model: Demo Phone (exemplar device)   Measured L × W × T (mm): 150.0 × 72.0 × 8.0
Also measured: camera bump 30.0 × 30.0 × 1.5, 8.0 from the top and side edges; buttons stand 0.5 proud; corner radius 10.0. Each value is the average of 3 caliper trials.
Problem statementWhat is the problem? Phones are damaged when they are carried, dropped and pressed against keys and other objects. Cracked screens, scratched camera lenses and damaged ports are the most common repairs. Who has the problem? Phone owners, including almost every student at our school. Where? In pockets, backpacks, bags and on desks, everywhere a phone goes. Why is it important? Repairs are expensive, and a phone that doesn't work cuts a student off from school, family and safety tools.
Design statementDesign, model in Fusion and 3D-print a two-component, snap-together protective case for the Demo Phone. By Wed, Dec 16, deliver proof of concept: a printed prototype that fits the phone and has been tested against criteria 1–8 below.
Criteria (given)
  1. Two components joined by a mechanism (hinge, snap or slide). Each partner models one component. weight 2
    Ours: a slide-on cap with two snap detents.
  2. Fast to use: an average user can insert and secure the phone in 7 seconds or less, and remove it without tools. weight 3
    Tested as the average of 5 users.
  3. Protects: a raised lip stands at least 1.0 mm above the screen and at least 0.5 mm above the camera bump. The case doesn't break or come apart in a pants pocket or loose in a bag. weight 3
  4. Openings (these replace the earbud case's vent holes): every button, port, speaker and camera can be used with the case on, and the phone can't slip out through any opening. weight 2
  5. Fits: each interior dimension equals the measured phone dimension plus your stated clearance. The phone doesn't rattle and doesn't need force to go in. weight 3
    Our stated clearance: 0.4 mm per side and 0.4 mm above the screen.
Criteria (yours)

Add at least one size criterion (maximum outside dimensions) and at least two more measurable criteria (mass, wall thickness, cost, grip, stand angle, and so on).

  1. Size: outside dimensions no larger than 78 × 156 × 12 mm, so it still fits a jeans pocket. weight 2
  2. Material and time: no more than 35 g of PLA and no more than 4 h total print time, printed with no supports. This is stricter than the 50 g / 5 h limit, so a reprint still fits the schedule. weight 2
  3. Retention: the cap stays on in 5 of 5 one-meter drops onto a hard floor, tested with the printed dummy phone only, never a real one. weight 2

Weights: 3 = must have, 2 = important, 1 = nice to have. The decision matrix uses the same weights.

Constraints Time: the design is submitted for critical review by Fri, Dec 4. The 3D-printed prototype is submitted by Mon, Dec 7, with one reprint allowed by Fri, Dec 11. Final presentation: Wed, Dec 16.
Production: the maximum prototyping material is 50 g of PLA (about 40 cm³) per case, both components combined. The maximum print time is 5 hours per case. Record both from the slicer.
Safety: never drop-test or force-test a real phone. For impact or pocket tests, use a dummy block that is the same size as the phone.
Our slicer result: 3 h 40 m and about 31 g (25.1 cm³), Bambu A1, 0.20 mm Standard, PLA.
DeliverablesProof of concept: three or more concept sketches · a decision matrix with written justification · Fusion 3D models of both components plus the assembly · a multiview drawing of each component · a tolerance worksheet · a 3D-printed prototype · material volume and print time · a fit test with the real phone · a proof of concept statement · a project reflection · the final presentation.

Why this brief scores a 4 on rubric criterion 1

  • Every blank in the given criteria is filled with a number. “Protects” became “1.0 mm above the screen, 0.5 mm above the camera.”
  • The added criteria include the required size criterion plus two more, and each one can be checked with a ruler, a scale, a slicer or a test count.
  • Every criterion is weighted, and the same weights drive the decision matrix and the final scorecard.
  • The design statement says what will be delivered and by when, and it is limited to proof of concept.
