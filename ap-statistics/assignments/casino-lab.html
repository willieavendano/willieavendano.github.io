The big question. Every game in this lab is rigged — slightly. The house edge is only 3–6 cents per chip. So how many rounds does it take before you can actually see the edge? You will answer it three ways: play a small sample by hand, simulate thousands of rounds in Google Sheets, and calculate the exact probabilities. House rules (all three games)

  • One partner is the Player, the other is the House. Swap roles halfway through each game.
  • Every round, the Player bets exactly 1 chip. No raising, folding, or choices — every rule below is mechanical, so every round is a chance process we can model.
  • Record the Player's net result for every round: +2, +1, 0 (push — the chip comes back), or −1. Chips just make it feel real; your record is the data.
  • Each Player starts with 20 chips. If the House runs short, write an IOU and keep playing.

Warm-up — independence refresher

Conditional probability. P(A | B) is the probability of A given that B happened: P(A | B) = P(A and B) / P(B) In a table or simulation: count the trials where B happened, then find the fraction of those where A also happened.
Independent events. A and B are independent if knowing B happened does not change the chance of A: P(A | B) = P(A) Then (and only then) P(A and B) = P(A)·P(B). Dice have no memory. A deck does — every card dealt is a card that is gone.

Game 2 — Blackjack: Mimic the Dealer

Card values: 2–10 count face value; J, Q, K count 10; an Ace counts 11 unless that would put the hand over 21, then it counts 1.

  1. Shuffle the full deck before every hand.
  2. Deal two cards to the Player, two to the House (one face up).
  3. Player: takes cards until the total is 17 or more. No choices.
  4. Player over 21 = bust: −1 immediately, even if the House would bust later.
  5. House: takes cards until its total is 17 or more (it stands on every 17).

Day 2 build — poker and blackjack

Poker tab: 5,000 hands, dealt two ways

The gray engine deals every hand twice. The correct deal (columns B–G) draws 5 different cards — without replacement, like a real deck. The bad deal (columns M–R) uses RANDBETWEEN(1,52) for each card — with replacement, the shortcut from W3. Look at a few rows of each before you build.

  1. Pay every hand. VLOOKUP finds the hand's name in the paytable (Y4:Z12) and returns the net: H2 =VLOOKUP(G2,$Y$4:$Z$12,2,FALSE) S2 =VLOOKUP(R2,$Y$4:$Z$12,2,FALSE)
  2. Does card 2 match card 1's rank? A 1/0 indicator — its average is a proportion: K2 =IF(I2=J2,1,0) W2 =IF(U2=V2,1,0) Fill H, K, S, W down to row 5001 (copy row 2, Name Box H3:H5001, paste — and so on).
  3. Paytable summary (rows 4–12). Column AA gives the exact number of 5-card hands of each type (out of 2,598,960): AB4 =AA4/2598960 AC4 =COUNTIF($G$2:$G$5001,Y4) AD4 =AC4/5000 AE4 =COUNTIF($R$2:$R$5001,Y4) AF4 =AE4/5000 Fill down to row 12; row 13 totals are your check.
  4. Expected value: AB15 =SUMPRODUCT(AB4:AB12,Z4:Z12), then AB16 =AVERAGE(H2:H5001) and AB17 =AVERAGE(S2:S5001).
  5. With vs. without replacement (rows 20–22). Fill all four columns: simulated correct deal, simulated bad deal, and the exact values — =3/51 and =4/52 for row 20; =1-1317888/2598960 and =1-(13*12*11*10*9)/13^5 for row 21 (one minus “all five ranks different”); 0 and =1-(52*51*50*49*48)/52^5 for row 22. For the simulated row 21, add the counts of the hands that contain a repeated rank (one pair, two pair, three of a kind, full house, four of a kind) and divide by 5000.

Blackjack tab: two strategies, same cards

Each row is one hand. The engine plays it twice with the same cards: Strategy A hits until 17+ (your table's rule) and Strategy B hits until 12+ (“never bust”). Comparing on identical cards is a matched-pairs design — chance differences in the cards cancel out. Cards are dealt with replacement on purpose: casinos deal from a 6-deck shoe, where one card barely changes the odds (analysis question 7).

  1. Indicator and difference columns: H2 =IF(G2>21,1,0) K2 =I2-J2 Fill both down to row 5001.
  2. Outcome table (N8:O12). Strategy A uses column I, Strategy B column J: N8 =COUNTIF($I$2:$I$5001,">0")/5000 N9 =COUNTIF($I$2:$I$5001,0)/5000 N10 =COUNTIF($I$2:$I$5001,"<0")/5000 N11 =AVERAGE($I$2:$I$5001) N12 =COUNTIF($E$2:$E$5001,">21")/5000 (P(player busts) uses the totals in E; B uses F) Then N14 =AVERAGE(K2:K5001).
