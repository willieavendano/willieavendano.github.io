## Course Description

AP Statistics follows the College Board's **Fall 2026 Course and Exam Description**. Students learn to collect, analyze, and draw conclusions from data across four big ideas: exploring data, sampling and experimentation, probability and simulation, and statistical inference.

The course is investigative: nearly every topic begins with a question worth answering and real data to answer it with. The AP Exam is fully digital, taken in the Bluebook application, on **Tuesday, May 11, 2027 at 12:00 PM**.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. Explore and analyze data
2. Sample and experiment
3. Anticipate patterns using probability and simulation
4. Make statistical inferences
5. Use statistical tools
6. Apply statistical concepts to real-world scenarios
7. Communicate statistical findings
8. Think critically about data
9. Choose appropriate statistical methods

## Units

### Unit 1 — Exploring One-Variable Data and Collecting Data

- Investigative questions and variables
- Graphs for categorical and quantitative data
- Describing and comparing distributions
- Random sampling and experimental design

### Unit 2 — Probability, Random Variables, and Probability Distributions

- Two categorical variables and simulation
- Probability, conditional probability, and independence
- Random variables
- The binomial and normal distributions; the CLT

### Unit 3 — Inference for Categorical Data: Proportions

- Estimators and sampling distributions
- Confidence intervals for a proportion
- Significance tests and p-values; errors
- Two proportions; chi-square tests

### Unit 5 — Regression Analysis (taught before Unit 4)

- Scatterplots, correlation, and regression
- Residuals and least squares

### Unit 4 — Inference for Quantitative Data: Means

- Sampling distributions for means
- Tests for a mean
- Two means: intervals and tests

Week-by-week pacing — including which weeks carry no new content — is on the course page.

## Grades and Evaluations

*Grades will be calculated using the 4.0 scale as mentioned in the student handbook.*

**Summative Assessments (70%)** — The goal of these assessments is to evaluate mastery of content and skills. In this course, these include (but are not limited to) unit tests, the December data investigation, the AP-style practice exam, and the post-exam capstone project.

**Formative Assessments (25%)** — The goal of formative assessments is to gather feedback on student learning. Here students are working toward their mastery of content and skills. In this course, these include (but are not limited to) problem sets, FRQ practice, AP Classroom Progress Checks, class discussions, and unannounced quizzes.

**Diagnostics (5%)** — The goal of diagnostic assessments is to verify and evaluate knowledge that is not subject to being summative or formative evaluations. These include (but are not limited to) entrance or exit tickets.

### Description of Common Assessments

**Unit tests** mirror the AP Exam: multiple-choice questions plus free-response questions scored on the College Board's rubric. No extra time will be given unless there is approved accommodation.

**Free-response practice** is continuous. AP Statistics is graded on communication as much as computation — a correct number with no justification earns little credit, so we practice writing the reasoning out from day one.

**The data investigation and capstone** are your own statistical studies: pose a question, design the collection, analyze honestly, and present the conclusion with its limitations stated.

**Classwork (CW in Veracross)** should be completed during the allotted class time and is **graded for accuracy**. Since these assessments are formative, students will have the chance to resubmit work within an allotted time determined by the teacher.

**Homework (HW in Veracross)** assignments allow students to practice their skills outside of the school setting or prepare themselves for the following class. Homework assignments are due at the start of the class period.

Scoring on homework will be as follows:

* **4** — All components of the assignment are completed in full, addressing all requirements with a high level of detail.
* **3** — Most components of the assignment are completed, meeting most of the requirements with satisfactory detail.
* **2** — Some components of the assignment are completed, but gaps or missing elements are noticeable. Detail is lacking in some areas.
* **1** — Few components of the assignment are completed, and significant gaps or missing elements are evident. Details are minimal.
* **NTI** — No assignment is turned in and is subject to the late work policy.

A student who does not comply with this expectation is not showing respect for themselves, their classmates, or their teacher and will be in violation of the classroom rule. ## Using AI in this class

1. **Tutor, not ghostwriter.** Use it to explain, quiz you, or debug your thinking — not to produce the thing you turn in.

2. **Disclose it.** Name the tool and what it did, right on the work. Undisclosed AI is treated as plagiarism.

3. **You must be able to explain every line.** If you cannot defend it at the board, it is not yours.

4. **Verify.** AI invents numbers, citations, and units. Check against the source; units still travel with the math.

5. **Assessments are AI-free** unless the brief says otherwise.
