Read “given that” as a change to the denominator, not as decoration. Students commonly use the union rule for an “and” question or report a conditional probability with the original population in mind. Ask students to say the reference group aloud: “among the people who…”

Worked Example · Setup

After-school activities: an “or” event

At a school, P(A) = 0.42 for athletics, P(M) = 0.35 for music, and P(A ∩ M) = 0.18.

Question

What is the probability that a randomly selected student participates in athletics or music?

“Or” includes the overlap unless the question says otherwise.

This is a deliberately small example so the focus stays on translation and context. Before showing the worked table, ask students whether athletics and music are mutually exclusive. The given overlap makes the answer no. Do not let students add 0.42 and 0.35 without subtracting the double-counted group.

Worked Example · KDEA

P(A ∪ M), in context

StepWhat you write
KP(A) = 0.42, P(M) = 0.35, P(A ∩ M) = 0.18; one student is selected
DP(A ∪ M), the probability the student participates in athletics or music
EUse the addition rule: P(A ∪ M) = P(A) + P(M) - P(A ∩ M)
AP(A ∪ M) = 0.42 + 0.35 - 0.18 = 0.59. There is a 0.59 probability, or 59%, that a randomly selected student participates in athletics or music.

KDEA in statistics: compute and conclude in context.

Make the stats mapping explicit: K is data, design, and conditions; D is the probability or claim sought; E is the procedure or formula; A is the computation plus a conclusion in context. The most important correction is the final sentence. An answer without context is like an answer without units.

Probability · 2 of 3

Conditional probability changes the denominator

Unconditional

P(A)

Share of the full population in event A.

Conditional

P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B)

Share in A among the cases already in B.

Independence means the condition does not change the probability: P(A | B) = P(A).

Use a two-way table or a verbal denominator check if students confuse independence with mutually exclusive events. “Independent” means knowing B does not change the chance of A; “mutually exclusive” means A and B cannot happen together. Those are not interchangeable ideas.

Practice · Turn & Talk · 8 minutes

Design a simulation for a probability

No answer key on this slide

A game has a 0.30 probability of a win on each round. Estimate the probability of winning at least 4 of 10 rounds.

  1. Describe one trial, including a random device or tool.
  2. Describe how one repetition produces a yes/no outcome.
  3. State what you would record across many repetitions.

Use StatKey or a short R/Python simulation; name the event being counted.

Do not reveal a simulation recipe or numerical answer here. Listen for a complete design: generate ten independent outcomes with win probability 0.30, count whether the total is at least 4, and repeat many times. Students often simulate one round only, or report the proportion of wins rather than the proportion of repetitions meeting the event. StatKey is useful for visual repetition; R or Python can make the logic reproducible.

Act II · Random Variables

Turn outcomes into a distribution.

Define X before you calculate with it.

The bridge here is definition. A random variable is not just a letter; it is a rule assigning a number to each outcome. Students who skip that definition often confuse a count, a proportion, and an individual outcome later in the unit.

Worked Example · KDEA

Expected number of late buses

Let X be the number of late buses observed in a randomly chosen day. The distribution has values 0, 1, 2 with probabilities 0.50, 0.35, 0.15.

StepWhat you write
KX = number of late buses in one day; P(X = 0) = 0.50, P(X = 1) = 0.35, P(X = 2) = 0.15
DμX, the long-run mean number of late buses per day
EμX = ΣxP(x)
AμX = 0(0.50) + 1(0.35) + 2(0.15) = 0.65. Over many days, the mean number of late buses is 0.65 per day.
The multiplication by each value is the conceptual step: the mean is a weighted average, not the middle listed value. The common failure is reporting “0.65 buses today.” Keep the conclusion long-run and in context. The exact arithmetic here uses =; reserve ≈ for rounded results.

Random Variables · 2 of 3

Binomial: count successes in a fixed set of trials

BINS
Conditions
Binary outcomes; fixed Independent trials; fixed Number of trials; same Success probability.
X
Model
X = number of successes; \( X \sim \text{Binomial}(n, p) \); \( \mu = np \); \( \sigma = \sqrt{np(1-p)} \).

“At least” and “at most” describe ranges of counts. Write the range before using technology.

The BINS mnemonic is a support, not a substitute for explaining independence in context. Ask whether sampling is with replacement or whether the 10% condition supports treating trials as independent. A frequent error is using a binomial model for a variable that is not a count of successes.

Practice · Individual then Pair · 10 minutes

Model selection: binomial or not?

No answer key on this slide
  1. A quality-control test labels each of 12 items acceptable or not, with a constant 0.08 defect probability.
  2. A student records the number of text messages received during a randomly selected hour.
  3. For each situation: define X, identify whether a binomial model is reasonable, and justify each BINS condition that applies.

Do the conditions in words before opening a calculator.

Do not reveal answers. The first setting has a natural fixed number of binary trials and a stated constant probability. The second is a count, but not automatically binomial: there is no fixed number of success/failure trials as stated. Students often see “number of” and immediately choose binomial. Require one sentence per condition, not only the mnemonic.

Worked Example · KDEA

Battery life: a probability below a cutoff

StepWhat you write
KX ∼ Normal(μ = 18, σ = 2.5) hours; cutoff x = 20 hours
DP(X < 20), the probability a randomly selected battery lasts less than 20 hours
EUse the normal CDF for the area to the left of 20 hours
Az = (20 - 18) / 2.5 = 0.80; P(X < 20) ≈ 0.788. Approximately 78.8% of modeled batteries last less than 20 hours.

The sentence names the batteries, the cutoff, and the direction.

Model the habit of writing the event before the calculator command. Accept calculator values such as 0.7881, but require a reasonable rounded statement. The common failure is saying “the probability is 78.8 hours” or stopping at 0.788 with no reference to battery life.

Act III · Sampling Distributions

A statistic is a random variable too.

Sample-to-sample variation is the bridge to inference.

This is the conceptual turn students need before the final review. The sample mean or sample proportion changes from sample to sample, so it has a sampling distribution. The CLT is about the shape of that distribution under conditions, not about making every raw data set normal.

Sampling Distributions · 1 of 2

Track the statistic, not the individual

p̂
Sample proportion
If samples come from a population with proportion p, then \( \mu_{\hat{p}} = p \) and \( \sigma_{\hat{p}} = \sqrt{p(1-p)/n} \).
x̄
Sample mean
If samples come from a population with mean μ and SD σ, then \( \mu_{\bar{x}} = \mu \) and \( \sigma_{\bar{x}} = \sigma/\sqrt{n} \).

Increasing n makes the statistic less variable; it does not automatically remove bias.

Keep population symbols and sample symbols separate. Students frequently use the standard deviation of individual observations when asked about the sample mean. Have them say “typical distance of sample means” for the standard error. Also emphasize that larger n reduces variability, not necessarily bias.

Worked Example · KDEA

Sampling proportions: a distribution of p̂

StepWhat you write
KPopulation proportion p = 0.40; random sample n = 100; independent observations are reasonable
Dstandard deviation of the sample proportion p̂
E\( \sigma_{\hat{p}} = \sqrt{p(1-p)/n} \)
Aσp̂ = √[(0.40)(0.60) / 100] ≈ 0.049. Across random samples of 100, sample proportions typically vary by about 0.049 from the population proportion.

The unit is proportion points, not individual people.

This example is about interpretation as much as substitution. The standard deviation is a measure of sample-to-sample variation in p-hat. Students often call 0.049 “4.9 people”; keep the statistic and its scale visible. Ask which assumptions support the formula before using it.
