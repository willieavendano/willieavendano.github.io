## A. Theoretical frameworks

1. **Mulvey, L. (1975). "Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema." *Screen* 16(3): 6–18.** DOI 10.1093/screen/16.3.6. The baseline "male gaze" argument; her categories (to-be-looked-at-ness, fetishistic scopophilia, sadistic voyeurism) map directly onto framing/lighting motifs. Must engage, even to depart from it.
2. **Mulvey, L. (1996). "Netherworlds and the Unconscious: Oedipus and *Blue Velvet*." In *Fetishism and Curiosity* (BFI/Indiana UP), ch. 9, pp. 137–154.** Mulvey herself on Lynch; lets you pit Mulvey-on-Lynch against later feminist readings. Reissued 2019 as *Fetishism and Curiosity: Cinema and the Mind's Eye*.
3. **Doane, M. A. (1982). "Film and the Masquerade: Theorising the Female Spectator." *Screen* 23(3–4): 74–88; reprinted in *Femmes Fatales* (Routledge, 1991).** Masquerade and femme fatale: the lens for Dorothy, Alice/Renee, Rita/Camilla; vocabulary for "performed" femininity as agency or its absence.
4. **Creed, B. (1988). "A Journey Through *Blue Velvet*: Film, Fantasy and the Female Spectator." *New Formations* 6.** Asks directly "whether the female in film can be afforded her own gaze." Pair with **Creed (1993) *The Monstrous-Feminine* (Routledge)** for abjection and the maternal.
5. **de Lauretis, T. (1984). *Alice Doesn't: Feminism, Semiotics, Cinema* (Indiana UP).** Narrative positions women as obstacle/space; useful for asking whether the woman becomes the narrative's subject by *Mulholland Drive*.
6. **Kaplan, E. A. (1983). *Women and Film: Both Sides of the Camera* (Methuen; Routledge reissue 2005).** Accessible synthesis; the "is the gaze male?" chapter is a good first read before Mulvey.
7. **Silverman, K. (1988). *The Acoustic Mirror: The Female Voice in Psychoanalysis and Cinema* (Indiana UP).** Lynch's lip-sync and voice/body splits (Club Silencio, Dorothy singing, Julee Cruise) are agency questions about *voice*, not just image.
8. **Butler, J. (1990). *Gender Trouble* (Routledge).** Agency as resignification within repetition; a non-psychoanalytic definition of agency that can be operationalized (does the character repeat, refuse, or reroute a scripted gesture?).
9. **Gill, R. (2007). "Postfeminist Media Culture: Elements of a Sensibility." *European Journal of Cultural Studies* 10(2): 147–166.** DOI 10.1177/1367549407075898. The media-studies construct of "agency/choice" and its critique; helps define autonomy/vulnerability/resistance/control as coded categories rather than impressions.

## B. Lynch-specific scholarship on women, gender, gaze

1. **Nochimson, M. P. (1997). *The Passion of David Lynch: Wild at Heart in Hollywood* (U Texas Press).** Chapters on *Blue Velvet*, *Twin Peaks*, *FWWM*; argues Lynch subverts Hollywood gender roles. The optimistic pole of the debate.
2. **Nochimson, M. P. (2013). *David Lynch Swerves: Uncertainty from Lost Highway to Inland Empire* (U Texas Press).** Covers the back half of the corpus.
3. **Nochimson, M. P. (2004). "'All I Need Is the Girl'…" in Sheen & Davison (below).** Chapter title confirmed; the subtitle "The Life and Death of Creativity in *Mulholland Drive*" is **UNVERIFIED** (check the book's TOC).
4. **Sheen, E. & Davison, A. (eds.) (2004). *The Cinema of David Lynch: American Dreams, Nightmare Visions* (Wallflower, Directors' Cuts).** Also contains **Jana Evans Braziel, "'In Dreams…': Gender, Sexuality and Violence in the Cinema of David Lynch"** (verified) and an Anne Jerslev chapter on *Lost Highway* (exact title **UNVERIFIED**).
5. **McGowan, T. (2004). "Lost on Mulholland Drive: Navigating David Lynch's Panegyric to Hollywood." *Cinema Journal* 43(2): 67–89.** DOI 10.1353/cj.2004.0008. Expanded in **McGowan (2007). *The Impossible David Lynch* (Columbia UP)**: fantasy/desire structure across all the films.
6. **Chion, M. (1995; 2nd ed. 2006). *David Lynch* (BFI).** Translation credits vary across records. Best on sound and the "voice" motifs.
7. **Layton, L. (1994). "*Blue Velvet*: A Parable of Male Development." *Screen* 35(4): 374–393.** DOI 10.1093/screen/35.4.374. The strongest statement that *Blue Velvet* is about Jeffrey, not Dorothy; a foil for any claim that Dorothy has agency.
8. **George, D. H. (1995). "Lynching Women: A Feminist Reading of *Twin Peaks*." In D. Lavery (ed.), *Full of Secrets: Critical Approaches to Twin Peaks* (Wayne State UP).** Argues the show "breaks women in half."
9. **Lafky, S. (1999). "Gender, Power, and Culture in the Televisual World of *Twin Peaks*: A Feminist Critique." *Journal of Film and Video* 51(3/4): 5–19.** (Semantic Scholar mis-titles it "Genre.")
10. **Love, H. (2004). "Spectacular Failure: The Figure of the Lesbian in *Mulholland Drive*." *New Literary History* 35(1): 117–132.** DOI 10.1353/nlh.2004.0021.
11. **Hudson, J. A. (2004). "'No Hay Banda, and yet We Hear a Band': David Lynch's Reversal of Coherence in *Mulholland Drive*." *Journal of Film and Video* 56(1): 17–24.** JSTOR 20688440. Relevant to the voice/lip-sync motif.
12. **Schaffner, A. K. (2009). "Fantasmatic Splittings and Destructive Desires: Lynch's *Lost Highway*, *Mulholland Drive* and *Inland Empire*." *Forum for Modern Language Studies* 45(3): 270–291.** DOI 10.1093/fmls/cqp105. The closest existing cross-film study of the woman split into virgin/whore; position the project against this.
13. **Hallam, L. (2018). *Twin Peaks: Fire Walk with Me* (Auteur, Devil's Advocates).** Includes a chapter reading it as a trauma film; the anchor for the abuse-survival reappraisal.
14. **Grossman, J. & Scheibel, W. (2020). *Twin Peaks* (Wayne State UP, TV Milestones).** Ch. 3 on femmes fatales and "female trauma and agency."
15. **Lyons, S. (2017). "Between Two Worlds: *Twin Peaks* and the Film/Television Divide." *Open Library of Humanities* 3(1): 8.** DOI 10.16995/olh.89. Open access, peer-reviewed, on *FWWM*'s reception.
16. **Bushman, D. & Ryan, S. (eds.) (2019). *The Women of David Lynch: A Collection of Essays* (Fayetteville Mafia Press).** Not peer-reviewed (fan press; intro by Philippa Snow, essay by Hallam); cite as reception, not scholarship.
17. **Mactaggart, A. (2010). *The Film Paintings of David Lynch: Challenging Film Theory* (Intellect).** Best on colour and painterly composition as method.
18. **Žižek, S. (2000). *The Art of the Ridiculous Sublime: On David Lynch's Lost Highway* (U Washington Press).** Lacanian; short.
19. **Olson, G. (2008). *David Lynch: Beautiful Dark* (Scarecrow).** Production context, not theory.
20. **Warner, M. (1997). "Voodoo Road." *Sight and Sound*, August 1997.** Full text free at bfi.org.uk. Doubling/possession in *Lost Highway*.
21. **Jerslev, A. (2021). *David Lynch: Blurred Boundaries* (Palgrave).** DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-73924-9. Recent; atmosphere and visual noise.
22. **Post-2025:** *Sight and Sound* March 2025 tribute issue; **Panoptikum** (U Gdańsk, open access) special issue "The Artistry and Legacy of David Lynch," guest ed. Ewa Mazierska, likely to appear in 2026; **Film Quarterly 80(1), Fall 2026, dossier "Mulholland Drive at 25"** (confirm against the issue). No *Screen*/*JCMS* special issue found.

Not found / UNVERIFIED: a peer-reviewed journal article specifically reading *FWWM* as an abuse-survival text post-2017; the closest are Hallam, Grossman & Scheibel, Lyons, and chapters in **Sanna, A. (ed.) (2019). *Critical Essays on Twin Peaks: The Return* (Palgrave)**, DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-04798-6. Stallings's *Laura's Ghost* (2020) is interviews, not scholarship.

## C. Method texts

1. **Bordwell, D., Thompson, K., & Smith, J. *Film Art: An Introduction*, 13th ed. (McGraw Hill, 2023).** Mise-en-scène and cinematography chapters supply the operational definitions for lighting, color, framing.
2. **Rose, G. *Visual Methodologies*, 5th ed. (SAGE, 2022).** The "compositional interpretation" and content analysis chapters bridge film-studies close reading and social-science coding.
3. **Schreier, M. *Qualitative Content Analysis in Practice* (SAGE, 2012).** The coding-frame build/pilot/revise procedure to follow and cite.
4. **Mayring, P. (2014). *Qualitative Content Analysis* (SSOAR, open access PDF).**
5. **Saldaña, J. *The Coding Manual for Qualitative Researchers*, 4th ed. (SAGE, 2021).** Codebook design and second-cycle coding.
6. **Krippendorff, K. *Content Analysis*, 4th ed. (SAGE, 2018).** Only if intercoder reliability (Krippendorff's alpha) is reported.
7. **Sandelowski, Voils & Knafl (2009). "On Quantitizing." *Journal of Mixed Methods Research* 3(3): 208–222.** DOI 10.1177/1558689809334210. Justifies counting your own codes.
8. **McHugh, M. L. (2012). "Interrater reliability: the kappa statistic." *Biochemia Medica* 22(3): 276–282.** Only if kappa is reported.
9. Aumont & Marie, *L'Analyse des films*: no English translation found; skip.

Access from Miami: check mdpls.org "Online Resources" with a Miami-Dade eCard. **UM Richter**: no remote database access for non-affiliates; in-building use via Friends of the Libraries membership (fee). **FIU**: visitors enter with government ID, in-library use only; borrowing needs Library Associates membership. ## F. Honest assessment

Lynch-and-women is crowded: four decades of psychoanalytic single-film readings (Mulvey, Creed, Layton on *Blue Velvet*; McGowan, Love, Hudson on *Mulholland Drive*), plus a post-2017 wave rehabilitating *FWWM* as a trauma film. What is genuinely thin is *systematic, cross-film, motif-level coding*: only Schaffner (2009) and Braziel (2004) read the corpus comparatively, and neither uses an explicit coding frame or tracks motifs like mirrors/lighting/color against defined agency categories. That is the defensible gap: a Schreier-style coding frame applied to a fixed sample of scenes, with results tabulated by film, framed as testing whether the "evolution" claimed by Nochimson and Schaffner holds at the level of the image. The three sources that cannot be skipped: **Mulvey 1975 (and her 1996 *Blue Velvet* essay)**, **Schaffner 2009** (the nearest prior study), and **Nochimson 1997/2013** (the strongest pro-agency reading, which the data will either support or complicate). Layton 1994 and Love 2004 are the next tier.
