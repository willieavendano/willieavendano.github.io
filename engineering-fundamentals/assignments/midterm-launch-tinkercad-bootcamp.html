Part 1 · Measuring with calipers

How to read the calipers. Close the jaws and press zero. Use the big outside jaws for the outside of a part (cap diameter), and the depth rod that slides out the end for how deep or tall something is. Hold the part square in the jaws, snug but not squeezed. Read to 0.01 mm, then write it to 0.1 mm. Measure three times in three different spots and average them.
FeatureTrial 1 (mm)Trial 2 (mm)Trial 3 (mm)Average (mm)
Bottle cap: diameter (outside jaws, across the crimps)
Bottle cap: height (how far the edge hangs down)

Key ring: inside diameter

Copy these averages into rows 1–3 of the Measurement Worksheet in your midterm packet. Why three trials? Caps aren't perfectly round and nobody holds calipers perfectly. If your three numbers are more than 0.3 mm apart, measure again.

Part 2 · Find the lever in a real opener

Use one of the class bottle openers. Put it on a capped bottle the way you'd use it, but don't open the bottle. Find the point where it rests on top of the cap (fulcrum), the edge of the tooth that hooks under the cap (load) and the middle of where your fingers push (effort). Measure both arms with the calipers or a ruler, from the fulcrum.

Worked example (made-up opener): load arm 28 mm, effort arm 70 mm. IMA = 70 ÷ 28 = 2.5. The opener multiplies your force by about 2.5, but your hand moves about 2.5 times farther than the cap edge does. Less force, more distance: the same tradeoff you saw with the ramps in 2.1.

The target

Step by step

  1. Log in. Go to tinkercad.com and join our class with the code Mr. Avendano gives you. Click Create → 3D Design. Rename it (click the random name at the top left) to Lastname_NameTag. ☐
  2. Look around. Right-click and drag to orbit. Scroll to zoom. Shift + right-drag (or middle-drag) to pan. Lost? Click the home icon above the zoom buttons on the left. ☐
  3. Check the units. Bottom right: Settings (Edit Grid) → Units: Millimeters. Every number today is in mm. ☐
  4. Place a box. From Basic Shapes on the right, drag a Box onto the workplane. ☐
  5. Size it exactly. Click the box. Click a white corner handle, then click each number that appears and type: length 50, width 20. Click the white handle on top and type height 4. Never drag to size; always type. ☐
  6. Round the corners. With the box selected, find the shape panel (top right). Set Radius to 3. ☐
  7. Make the ring hole. Drag a Cylinder onto the workplane. Size it 6 × 6 and make it taller than the box (height 10). In the shape panel, click Hole (or press H). It turns see-through gray. ☐
  8. Place the hole with the Ruler. Drag the Ruler (top right toolbar) to the left end of the box. Click the cylinder; the Ruler shows how far its edge is from the end of the box. Type 3, so the hole’s edge is 3 mm in and its center is 6 mm from the end (the hole is 6 mm across). To center it across the width, select both shapes (Shift-click), press L (Align), and click the middle dot on the width direction. ☐
  9. Add your initials. Drag a Text shape onto the workplane. In the shape panel, type your initials. Size it to fit on the right side of the tag (about 25 × 12 mm) and set its height to 1. ☐
  10. Lift the text onto the tag. Click the text. Drag the small black cone above it straight up, or click it and type 4, so it sits exactly on top of the 4 mm box. Check from the side view: no gap, no overlap. ☐
  11. Group it. Select everything (Ctrl/⌘ + A) and press Group (Ctrl/⌘ + G). The hole cuts through the tag and it all becomes one part. Spin it around: can you see through the ring hole? ☐
  12. Measure and screenshot. Use the Ruler to check the length, width and total height (should be 5 mm). Take two screenshots, from the top and the side, with the Ruler showing. Paste both into your notebook. ☐
  13. Export. Click Export (top right) → .STL. Name the file Lastname_NameTag.stl and upload it where Mr. Avendano tells you. ☐
Done early? Try these (each one is a move you'll want for your opener):
  • Cut-in text instead of raised: make the text a Hole, height 2, sitting 1 mm down into the tag (elevation 3). Group again.
  • A custom outline: group a box and a cylinder into a shape like a guitar pick or a shield, then add the ring hole.
  • Duplicate and mirror: Ctrl/⌘ + D duplicates a shape. Try the Mirror tool (M) on your text and see what happens.
