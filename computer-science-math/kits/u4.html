Concept

Deck Returns & Volatility not built yet

A return is percent change; volatility is how much those changes scatter. Together they are the two numbers that describe any asset — and the inputs to every forecast you will build.

Deck Monte Carlo Forecasting not built yet

You cannot predict one future, so simulate a thousand. The casino's tools aimed at your own portfolio: a distribution of outcomes instead of a single misleading number — and a way to judge your Investopedia trades that is not just "did it go up".

Practice

Research, Returns & Simulation Practice — csm-p4-stocks-practice.xlsx

  1. Open the Investopedia simulator and take one position. Record why you bought it BEFORE you know what happens.
  2. Pull a price history and compute daily returns as percent change.
  3. Build a moving average and explain what it hides as well as what it shows.
  4. Compute volatility as the standard deviation of returns — then compare a utility stock against a growth stock and describe the difference in one sentence.
  5. Run a one-year Monte Carlo forecast from those two numbers and chart the spread of outcomes.
  6. Report the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentile outcome — and explain why quoting only the median is dishonest.

The Challenge

The Investopedia $100,000 Challenge

Platform
Investopedia Stock Simulator (free account; the class joins one game)
Capital
$100,000 simulated

Rules

  1. One holding minimum in each assigned vertical — you may not put it all in tech.
  2. Every trade needs a recorded thesis BEFORE it is placed: why this company, what you expect, and what would prove you wrong.
  3. You are graded on the quality of the reasoning and the model behind it, not on whether you beat the market. A well-argued loss outscores a lucky win.
  4. No day trading. Positions are held and reviewed weekly.

Verticals — one holding minimum in each

  • Technologysoftware, semiconductors, hardware
  • Healthcarepharma, devices, insurers
  • Consumerretail, food and beverage, apparel
  • Energy & Utilitiesoil and gas, renewables, power
  • Financialsbanks, payments, insurance
  • Industrials & Transportmanufacturing, logistics, airlines

Build — what you hand in

Stocks: Forecasting & Management (Thu Feb 11)

A managed $100,000 Investopedia portfolio with at least one holding in each assigned vertical, plus the Excel model behind it: real price data, computed returns and volatility per holding, a Monte Carlo forecast with a percentile band, and a written defence of your allocation that was recorded BEFORE the outcome was known.

Stretch (optional). Add correlation between two holdings and show how it changes the spread of outcomes — then say whether your actual portfolio was diversified or only looked diversified.
