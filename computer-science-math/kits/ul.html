Concept

Deck Expected Value & the House Edge

Expected value is what one play is worth on average — and the house edge is why 'on average' beats 'lucky' over a long enough night. This is the bridge to every simulation that follows.

Practice

Simulation Practice — csm-pl-casino-practice.xlsx

  1. RAND and RANDBETWEEN: simulate one coin flip, then 10, then 1,000.
  2. Compute expected value for a simple bet by hand, then simulate it and compare.
  3. Chart the running average across 1,000 trials and watch it settle — the law of large numbers, seen.
  4. Model a real casino game's house edge and compute expected loss per hour of play.

Explore

Law of Large Numbers Simulator

Run 10, 100, 1,000, or 100,000 trials of a chosen bet and watch the running average converge on expected value in real time. The point students cannot get from a static deck: small samples lie, large ones do not.

Build before Cornerstone IV — the Monte Carlo work in Stocks assumes this intuition is already in place.

Build — what you hand in

Casino Lab demo (Thu Jan 14)

The Casino Lab workbook: Field Dice, Match Poker and Blackjack each modelled in Google Sheets from the published rules (cards dealt from a shuffled deck, payouts and strategy in parameter cells), simulated thousands of times with a convergence chart, and compared on one Dashboard — exact EV where a formula exists, simulated edge where it does not, and the expected cost per hour. Presented live on Jan 14 with a one-page write-up.

Stretch (optional). Add straights and flushes to Match Poker and re-price the paytable; sweep the blackjack stand number 12–18 and chart the edge; simulate a 20-chip bankroll to see how often a player goes broke within an hour; or design a house game with a 1–3% edge and prove it two ways.

Rubric — Casino Lab demo

Scored on the house four-level scale: each criterion 1–4 (half levels allowed), grade = the average. Same criteria and descriptors as the student handout.
