Concept

Deck I Interest Works for You First

Mon Nov 2 + Tue Nov 3. Car research becomes inputs; reading a savings-account disclosure; why an APY converts to a monthly rate with (1+APY)^(1/12)-1 and a loan's contract rate with rate/12; building and checking the savings schedule; one-change-at-a-time what-if tests.

Deck II The Amortization Machine

Thu Nov 5, first half. Savings become a capped down payment; APR versus contract interest rate; the level-payment formula and PMT worked in KDEA; zero-rate and fully-saved edge cases; the amortization table, its three checks and the common bugs.

Deck III Compare, Own, Defend

Thu Nov 5, second half, and Tue Nov 10. 60 vs 72 months; a one-change scenario table; monthly ownership costs compared over the same time span; the model's honest limits; presentation structure, recommendation frame and the peer audit for Nov 12.

Build — what you hand in

Financing a Car (Thu Nov 12)

A model that takes the out-the-door price, down payment (from your savings plan), annual contract rate, and term as inputs and produces the monthly payment, a full amortisation schedule, and a defended recommendation of which financing option you would actually take.

Stretch (optional). Port the amortization schedule to Python (start from car_loan_amortization.py, which takes the term in years) and confirm both produce the same payment to the cent.

What each level looks like

The exemplar workbook csm-u2-exemplars.xlsx contains the same $12,000, 7%, 60-month loan built four times — one tab per level — plus a What Changed tab naming the specific difference between each pair. Read it before you start, not after you are graded. Its rate cell is labelled “APR”; with no finance fees that equals the contract rate used in this unit.
