What you start with

Your credit profile

Your private credit profile: job, salary, age, a simulated credit score (everyone is Prime), tier, history, utilization, late payments, open accounts — and the APRs a lender would offer you on 3- to 7-year new-car loans (Experian Q2 2026 tier averages, set by your score). You already priced a $36,000 loan ($40,000 − $4,000 down) at each term. Now swap that made-up car for a real one.

The rules

Same for everyone
  1. A real car in Miami-Dade. A car listed for sale in Miami-Dade County (dealer or private seller), out-the-door price $40,000 or less. Record URL, date viewed, screenshot, year/make/model/trim, mileage, new or used.
  2. Out-the-door (OTD) price by formula, never typed: listed price + Florida sales tax 6% + Miami-Dade surtax 1% on the first $5,000 only (Florida’s cap for a single item, so never more than $50) + dealer fee (no legal cap in Florida; use the listing’s or label an assumption, ~$999) + title & registration (labelled assumption, ~$400).
  3. Down payment of at least $4,000. Amount financed = OTD − down payment. A cheaper car still needs at least $4,000 down.
  4. Rates come from your credit profile. They are new-car rates. Used car? Add 2.66 percentage points to every term.
  5. Five loans, all by formula (3–7 years), a full amortization schedule for the term you choose, one chart, and a monthly cost to own block that feeds Cornerstone III. Details in the tabs table.
  6. A recommendation: which term you would take and why — payment, total interest, share of income, one limitation. Never call the smaller payment “cheaper.” It is smaller per month and more expensive in total.
Why at least $4,000 down?

$4,000 is 10% of the $40,000 ceiling. It is a rule about you, not the car:

  1. Depreciation. A car loses value fastest early. With little down you can owe more than it is worth (“upside down”) — ugly if it is totaled on the Palmetto.
  2. Loan-to-value. Lenders compare the loan to the car’s value; smaller is less risk.
  3. Smaller loan, less interest. Every dollar down is a dollar you never pay interest on.
  4. Proof you can save. If $4,000 is out of reach, the monthly costs will be too.
Why +2.66 points for a used car?

Your profile’s APRs are for new cars. A used car backing a loan is worth less, its value is harder to predict, and used-car loans default more often — so lenders charge more.

Experian, Q2 2026, Prime: new 6.15%, used 8.81%; gap 8.81 − 6.15 = 2.66 points. Class assumption: add it to every term.

Exemplar: had Camila bought used, her 5-year 5.62% would become 5.62 + 2.66 = 8.28%.
