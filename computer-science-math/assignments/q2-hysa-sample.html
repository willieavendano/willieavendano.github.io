From savings to a down payment

Student activity and spreadsheet starter instructions

All values are fictional classroom assumptions. Savings APY stays fixed; deposits occur at month-end; savings have no fees or withdrawals. Purchase price is held constant. The loan uses equal monthly periods, a fixed contract rate and no finance fees. Display rounds to cents; calculations keep full precision.

Sample recommendation

With the base inputs, 24 months of $150 deposits grows $500 to $4,302.33. Only $202.33 is interest; the regular deposits do most of the work. Allocating that entire balance to a $16,000 purchase leaves $11,697.67 to finance. At the assumed 7% contract rate, 60 months costs $231.63 per month and about $2,200.00 in interest. Extending to 72 months lowers the payment to $199.43 but increases interest to $2,661.55. If the higher payment fits after insurance, fuel, maintenance and reserves, the 60-month term reduces financing cost. This does not establish personal affordability: income and ownership costs are not included. A falling APY, missed deposit or higher future car price could change the plan.

This written recommendation describes the original sample inputs; the live tables update when you change inputs. Write a new recommendation for your own case.

Formula map

Savings monthly rate = (1 + APY)1/12 − 1. Next balance = previous balance × (1 + monthly rate) + deposit. Down payment = minimum of savings and purchase price. Amount financed = purchase price − down payment. Monthly payment = P × r / [1 − (1+r)−n], with P/n when r = 0. Interest = sum of payments − P.
