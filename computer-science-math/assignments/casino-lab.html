The Casino Lab

Computer Science Math 2026–27 · in class Jan 5–12 · demo Thursday, January 14, 2027

Name
Partner
Period
Driving question. The dice are fair and the deck is honest. People walk out ahead every night. So how does the casino still get paid — and how much does an evening cost you?
The plan. Play three games by hand for a few minutes, then build a Google Sheet that plays each one thousands of times. Your model, not your luck, answers the question.
Supplied: two dice, one deck, and 20 chips per pair. You need: a laptop with Google Sheets and the starter workbook. Chips have no cash value; this is a math model, not a gambling activity.

Learning goals

By the demo you can…
  1. Turn a game’s rules into a spreadsheet model: one row per round, helper columns, and a paytable the formulas look up.
  2. Keep every assumption (payouts, bet, strategy) in a labelled parameter cell, so changing one cell re-prices the whole game.
  3. Deal cards without replacement, and catch a model that silently produces impossible hands.
  4. Compute expected value with SUMPRODUCT where the math allows it, and measure it by simulation where it does not.
  5. Chart a running average converging on expected value, and turn a house edge into dollars per hour.

1. The casino floor

Tue Jan 5 · about 30 min

Setup. One partner is the Player, the other is the House; swap roles halfway through each game. The Player starts with 20 chips; the House pays and collects from the bank. Every game is Player vs House — never partner vs partner — so every game has fixed rules and a real house edge. Record every round before the next one starts, and write down how many minutes each game took (Part 6 turns that into dollars per hour).

Game 1 · 15 rounds

Field Dice

Bet 1 chip. Roll two dice once. The sum decides everything — each sum has exactly one result.

Sum 2win +2
Sum 3, 4, 9, 10, 11win +1
Sum 12win +3
Sum 5, 6, 7, 8lose −1

You win on 7 different sums and lose on only 4. Is that a good deal? Guess now; prove it Thursday.

Game 2 · 10 rounds

Blackjack vs the Dealer

Bet 2 chips. Shuffle the whole deck before every round. Cards 2–10 count face value; J, Q, K count 10; an Ace counts 11 unless that would bust you, then 1.

  1. Deal 2 to the Player, 2 to the Dealer (the House).
  2. Natural (Ace + a 10-card): Player natural wins +3; Dealer natural wins for the House (−2); both = push.
  3. Player hits until 17 or more. Over 21 = bust, lose −2 at once.
  4. Dealer hits until 17 or more. Dealer bust = Player +2.
  5. Otherwise higher total wins 2 chips; a tie is a push (0).

A hand stops at 5 cards. Both sides follow fixed rules — no choices — so a spreadsheet can play it exactly the same way.

Game 3 · 10 rounds

Match Poker

Bet 1 chip. Shuffle, deal the Player 5 cards, read the paytable. No draws, no folding, no betting rounds. Only matching ranks count — straights and flushes are not on this table.

No match (5 different ranks)lose −1
One pairpush 0
Two pairwin +5
Three of a kindwin +9
Full house (3 + 2)win +20
Four of a kindwin +50

2. Build Field Dice

Thu Jan 7 · 35 min · tab: Dice

Import the starter into Google Sheets (File → Import → Upload, or open it from Drive and Save as Google Sheets), then make your own copy. Yellow cells are yours to build; blue cells are parameters; gray is given. Type your Part 1 rounds into the Physical Play tab first — its formulas reuse each game’s paytable, so your real rounds and your simulated rounds are scored by the same model.

2The paytable does the thinkingLookup · SUMPRODUCT
  1. Ways and probability. In the paytable, the number of ways to roll a sum s is 6 − |s − 7|. Build J4 with that, then K4 = J4/36; fill down. The check cell must read 36.
  2. Exact expected value. L17: multiply each probability by its payout and add — one SUMPRODUCT. House edge L18 = −EV ÷ bet.
  3. One round, row 2. Die 1 and Die 2 are each RANDBETWEEN(1,6); Sum adds them; Net looks the sum up in the paytable: =VLOOKUP(D2, $I$4:$L$14, 4, FALSE) The $ signs pin the table so it does not slide when you fill down. No IF chains, no typed payouts.
  4. Running total and average. F2 = E2, then F3 = F2 + E3 filled down; G2 = F2 / A2. (Why not SUM($E$2:E3)? At row 10,000 that re-adds 10,000 cells, every row, every recalculation.)
  5. Scale. Test at 20 rows. Fill to row 10,001 (10,000 rounds). Fill the results block.
  6. Chart. Line chart of the running average (column G) vs round. Add the exact EV as a flat reference line (a helper column equal to $L$17 works).
ways total = 36exact EV = −1/36 ≈ −0.0278 chipsP(player wins a round) = 44.4%10,000-round average: usually −0.051 to −0.005

Tune it. Change exactly one blue payout so the game becomes perfectly fair (EV = 0). Which cell, and to what? Then put it back.

3. Cards as numbers — and the bug hunt

Thu Jan 7 · 30 min · tabs: Deck, Poker

A card is a number from 1 to 52. Rank = MOD(card−1, 13) + 1 gives 1 (Ace) through 13 (King); Suit = INT((card−1)/13) + 1 gives 1–4. The Deck tab lists all 52 so you can check yourself.

The bug hunt. Build the poker deal the “obvious” way first: =RANDBETWEEN(1,52) in each of the five card cells B2:F2. Then add the duplicate check in Q2:

=IF(SUMPRODUCT(COUNTIF(B2:F2,B2:F2))>5, "DUPLICATE", "ok")

Build ranks, the match score and the net (Part 4, steps 2–4), fill to 5,000 rows, and look at the results block. Answer before you fix anything:

  1. How many of your 5,000 hands are impossible (the same card twice)?
  2. Your any-match share vs the exact 49.29%: What does this model say the casino’s profit is?
  3. In one sentence: what real-world rule did the model forget?

The fix: shuffle a fresh deck for every row and deal from the top. In Google Sheets one formula in B2 fills B2:F2 (clear C2:F2 first — the result spills into them), then fill B2 down:

=TRANSPOSE(ARRAY_CONSTRAIN(SORT(SEQUENCE(52), RANDARRAY(52), TRUE), 5, 1))

Read it inside-out: SEQUENCE(52) is the deck 1–52; SORT(…, RANDARRAY(52), TRUE) sorts it by 52 random numbers — a shuffle; ARRAY_CONSTRAIN(…, 5, 1) keeps the top 5; TRANSPOSE lays them across the row. The duplicate count must now be 0 on every recalculation.

4. Build Match Poker

Mon Jan 11 · 40 min · tab: Poker
4One number classifies the handHelper columns · COUNTIF · lookup
  1. Deal with the shuffle formula from Part 3.
  2. Ranks in G2:K2: =MOD(B2-1,13)+1, filled right.
  3. Match score in L2: =SUMPRODUCT(COUNTIF(G2:K2, G2:K2)). Each card counts how many cards share its rank (itself included), and the counts are added. Work out a pair by hand: the two paired cards count 2 each, the other three count 1 → 2+2+1+1+1 = 7. Every hand type gets its own score:
    No matchOne pairTwo pairThree of a kindFull houseFour of a kind
    579111317
  4. Hand and Net (M2, N2): VLOOKUP the score in the paytable (columns 2 and 5, exact match, $-locked).
  5. Exact EV. Probability = ways ÷ COMBIN(52,5) (that is 2,598,960 possible hands); EV = SUMPRODUCT of probabilities and payouts.
  6. Simulated share of each hand: COUNTIF of the score column ÷ hands. Compare with the exact probabilities — this check settles much faster than the money does.
  7. Running total, running average, chart — same as dice. Fill to 5,000 hands.
ways total = 2,598,960P(no match) = 50.71%P(one pair) = 42.26%exact EV = −0.0384 chips (edge 3.84%)0 duplicates

Expect a wild ride. A 5,000-hand average usually lands between −0.099 and +0.022 — it can even come out positive. Big rare payouts (a +50 four of a kind) make poker the highest-variance game of the three. That is the lesson, not a bug: compare the frequencies to check your model, and the long-run average to price the game.

5. Build Blackjack

Tue Jan 12 · 50 min · tab: Blackjack

Dice and poker have a formula for the exact answer. Blackjack doesn’t — the hitting rules make the possibilities branch too much. This is the game where simulation is the only practical tool, which is exactly why casinos used computers to price it.

5Fixed rules, built column by columnMixed references · nested IF
  1. Deal 10 cards in B2 (player B–F, dealer G–K): the Part 3 formula with 10 in place of 5. The player uses only as many of B–F as the rules call for; unused cards are never looked at. (Why that is still a fair deal: you’d get the same result dealing in any fixed order from a shuffled deck.)
  2. Values L2:U2: =MIN(MOD(B2-1,13)+1, 10) — Ace = 1 for now, J/Q/K = 10.
  3. Best total after 2, 3, 4, 5 cards (V2:Y2). An Ace becomes 11 if that does not bust the hand: =IF(AND(COUNTIF($L2:M2,1)>0, SUM($L2:M2)+10<=21), SUM($L2:M2)+10, SUM($L2:M2)) Fill it right: $L2 stays anchored while the end slides to N, O, P — the first 3, 4, then 5 cards.
  4. Player final (Z2): the first best total that reaches the stand number in the blue cell $AN$4, else the 5-card total: =IF(V2>=$AN$4, V2, IF(W2>=$AN$4, W2, IF(X2>=$AN$4, X2, Y2))) A bust stops the hand too, because anything over 21 is also ≥ 17.
  5. Dealer: repeat steps 3–4 on Q2:U2 with $AN$5 (AA2:AE2).
  6. Naturals: AF2 =V2=21, AG2 =AA2=21.
  7. Result (AH2), tested in this order: both naturals → Push; player natural → Blackjack; dealer natural → Dealer BJ; player over 21 → Bust; dealer over 21 → Win; then higher total wins, equal is a Push.
  8. Net (AI2) from the result and the blue cells: Blackjack = bet × natural payout; Win = +bet; Push = 0; anything else = −bet. No typed 2s or 3s.
  9. Running total and average, chart, fill to 5,000 rounds. The results block’s check cell must say TRUE.
player natural ≈ 4.6% of roundsedge (stand on 17) = 5.57%5,000-round average: usually −0.167 to −0.056 chips

Strategy experiment. Change only the blue “Player stands on” cell to 12, then 15, then back to 17. Record the simulated edge each time. Can 5,000 rounds tell 15 from 17? (Hint: the 5,000-round range above is about ±2.8 percentage points wide.) On demo day the class pools everyone’s rounds to find out.

6. Convergence, cost, and the Dashboard

Tue Jan 12 · 15 min · tab: Dashboard

The Dashboard puts the three games side by side. Every yellow cell is a link (=Dice!L17) or a formula — never a typed copy of a number.

  • Physical vs simulated vs exact: one row each, per game. Your 10–15 hand-played rounds will be far from the exact value. That is the point.
  • Dollars per hour = (chips bet per round) × ($ per chip) × (rounds per hour) × (house edge). Use the pace you timed in Part 1; if you didn’t time a game, use the blue assumption cell and say so.
  • Charts: one convergence chart per game (Parts 2, 4, 5) plus one column chart of house edge by game. Title them with the finding (“Blackjack costs the most per hour”), not the topic (“Blackjack chart”).

7. Desmos option (Field Dice only)

Optional · a second engine for the same model

Desmos is good at lists, so it can run the dice game too. Card games need a fresh shuffle per hand, which Desmos lists don’t do cleanly — keep poker and blackjack in Sheets. In a new graph at desmos.com/calculator, one line each:

N = 10000 a = floor(6 random(N, 1)) + 1 b = floor(6 random(N, 2)) + 1 s = a + b w = {s = 2: 2, s = 12: 3, 5 ≤ s ≤ 8: -1, 1} mean(w) mean(a) total({a = b: 1, 0}) / N k = 10 (make it a slider from 1 to 10000) mean(w[1...k])

random(N, seed) makes a list of N random numbers between 0 and 1; different seeds give independent dice. Self-check before you trust it: mean(a) should be near 3.5 and the doubles share near 0.167 (if it says 1, both dice used the same seed). Drag the slider k from 10 to 10,000 and watch mean(w[1...k]) settle toward −0.0278. Change the seeds to re-roll everything.
