Driving question. How does a savings plan change the amount you need to borrow for the car you researched?
Bring: your car listing and research notes, a calculator, and a blank Excel workbook or Google Sheet. Use classroom assumptions instead of personal bank or family financial information. This is a mathematical model; no account application or purchase is part of the assignment.

Learning goals

By Thursday you can…
  1. Turn a car listing into model inputs, including an out-the-door price built by formula.
  2. Read a savings-account disclosure and record APY, conditions, fees, insurance and access with a URL and date.
  3. Convert a constant APY to its equivalent monthly rate, and explain why that differs from a loan's rate ÷ 12.
  4. Build a month-by-month savings schedule that separates contributions from interest and passes the published checks.
  5. Link the savings balance to a down payment, build a loan payment and amortization schedule, and compare 60 vs 72 months.
  6. Write a recommendation that states monthly payment, total cost, ownership costs and one limitation.

3. The rate lesson

Mon · 10 min
3APY is not a rate to divide by 12Deck I, slides 6–8

APY (annual percentage yield) describes one year’s effective yield, including compounding. If a constant APY is a, use the equivalent monthly rate r=(1+a)^(1/12)-1. Enter 4.5% as 4.5% or 0.045, not 4.5 (that means 450%).

For a constant nominal annual interest rate j compounded monthly — how a loan contract states its rate — use j/12. Those are different inputs.

At the same fixed APY and with no transactions, monthly and daily equivalent rates give the same full-year growth. To study the effect of compounding frequency, hold the nominal annual rate fixed instead (4.5% nominal: monthly compounding gives an APY of about 4.594%; daily gives about 4.602%). Compare the same elapsed time, not 60 months against 60 days.

Worked conversion: APY 4.5%Value
Year growth factor1 + 0.045 = 1.045
Month growth factor = 1.0451/12≈ 1.0036748
Monthly rate r≈ 0.0036748 (about 0.367% per month)
Check: 1.003674812≈ 1.045 ✓

Predict: With $500 initially and $150 deposited monthly, will most of the money after two years come from your deposits or interest? Explain before calculating.

Create a sheet named Loan. The sample uses a $15,000 listed car plus $1,000 in assumed taxes and fees. Its $16,000 purchase price is held constant while you save, an assumption to challenge in your conclusion. Allocate the selected savings balance to a down payment, capped at the purchase price. Do not spend it twice or assume an emergency reserve is available for the car.

APR vs interest rate: APR includes certain borrowing fees; the contract interest rate drives this simplified monthly amortization. In our no-finance-fee sample they are equal. A lender’s quoted APR alone is not always the correct rate to plug into an exact payment schedule. Real auto loans may accrue daily; this model assumes equal monthly periods, on-time payments and no finance fees.

Edge cases: at a 0% rate the payment is B4 ÷ B6 (sample: about $194.96) and interest is $0. If savings exceed the price, the down payment is the full price and the amount financed is $0.
