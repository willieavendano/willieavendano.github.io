What you start with

Your hand

Your credit profile’s job and gross salary (W-2 employee, or self-employed if your profile says so) · your Cornerstone II workbook with the chosen loan’s payment and your monthly cost to own · a starting emergency fund (class default $3,000; a labelled assumption).

The rules

Same for everyone
  1. Take-home pay by formula. 2026 federal income tax, single filer, standard deduction, brackets — all in input cells, never inside formulas. FICA 7.65%. Florida has no state income tax: show the $0 line and say what it is worth (pick a state with an income tax, cite its rate, and compute what you would pay there).
  2. A real Miami-Dade apartment. URL, date viewed, screenshot, neighborhood, beds/baths, rent. Up to 2 roommates; your share = rent ÷ people unless you state another split. Show housing as % of gross monthly income against the 30% guideline. The class 17-neighborhood rent table is a reference, not a source.
  3. At least 12 spending categories, each labelled Fixed or Variable, each with a source or a labelled assumption. Car payment, car insurance, gas, maintenance and tolls come by live link from your CS II tabs, never retyped. Totals by SUMIF on Fixed/Variable. Savings = take-home − expenses; savings rate by formula (target ≥ 10%; if you miss it, report the gap in dollars).
  4. Emergency fund: a labelled starting balance (class default $3,000).
  5. Scenarios: baseline + at least three, side by side, each changing one thing: a Miami curveball (hurricane deductible, car repair on the 826, rent hike at renewal, insurance jump); a good-news case (raise or bonus); and one that breaks the budget (savings negative), with months until the emergency fund hits zero by formula and a stated, modelled fix. Downgrading the car or adding a roommate are legitimate fixes — show the numbers.
  6. Two charts: where the money goes (a bar per category, or stacked Fixed/Variable); emergency-fund balance over 12 months, baseline vs the breaking scenario. Labelled axes, $ axis from 0.
“My budget breaks — here is my fix” is an expected outcome, not a failure

Every real budget has a month it cannot survive. The grade is for finding that month honestly, measuring how long your cushion lasts, and modelling a fix that works — not for pretending the break cannot happen.

Take-home pay: the steps

Each rate applies only to the dollars inside its bracket. Tax in one bracket:
=rate*MAX(0,MIN(taxable,top)-bottom), then add the brackets.

Self-employed profile? No employer withholds FICA for you, so self-employment tax replaces it: SE tax = net earnings × 92.35% × 15.3%. Half of it is deductible before income tax: taxable = net − ½ SE tax − $16,100. Take-home = net − SE tax − income tax. Treat your profile salary as your net self-employment earnings. No employer health plan either: add a marketplace health premium to your budget (labelled assumption, about $375/mo).
