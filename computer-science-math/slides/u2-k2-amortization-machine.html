Definitions: CFPB, “What are the key terms I should know when shopping for an auto loan?”

Do not let "APR" and "interest rate" become synonyms. A lender's APR can be higher than the contract rate because it folds in fees; plugging an APR into PMT gives a schedule that does not match the contract. Our model has no finance fees, so the numbers coincide — but students should still label cell B5 "Annual contract interest rate." The exemplar workbook labels its 7% rate "APR"; under its no-fee setup they agree.

Part One

One Payment, Two Jobs

Every payment pays this month's interest first. Whatever is left pays down the balance.

The Payment · 1 of 3

The level-payment formula

\( M = \dfrac{P \, i}{1 - (1+i)^{-n}} \qquad i = \dfrac{j}{12} \)

P
Amount financed
j
Annual contract rate
i
Monthly rate = j / 12
n
Number of payments

Excel does the same math: =-PMT(B5/12,B6,B4)

Contrast with Tuesday: the savings APY needed the 12th root; the loan's contract rate is a nominal annual rate compounded monthly, so it is divided by 12. The minus sign in front of PMT is only sign convention — Excel reports money paid out as negative. Students who skip the minus sign get a negative payment and then a negative total interest.

The Payment · 3 of 3 · Edge cases

Make the formula survive strange inputs

CellLabelFormula
B5Annual contract interest rate7%
B6Term (positive whole months)60
B7Monthly payment=IF(B5=0,B4/B6,-PMT(B5/12,B6,B4))
B8Total loan interest=B7*B6-B4
B9Purchase + financing cost=B3+B7*B6
  • 0% promotion: the payment is \( P/n \) and interest is $0.
  • Savings cover the car: financed $0, payment $0.
At 0% the closed-form formula divides 0 by 0, so the IF branch is the honest fix (Excel's PMT happens to handle rate 0, but the formula on the board does not, and the Python stretch must branch anyway). Test: rate 0 gives 11,697.67 / 60 ≈ $194.96. Term must be a positive whole number; a term of 0 should be treated as invalid input, not a payment. This is the rubric's "edge cases behave sensibly" line.

  • Opening balance always comes from the previous closing balance.
Interest is opening balance × annual rate ÷ 12 — the contract rate, not the savings root formula. Most common bug: the opening balance column points at B4 in every row instead of the previous closing balance, so interest never shrinks. Second: forgetting $ on B5 or B7. A table that is longer than the term will run negative after the last payment — stop at the term.

The Schedule · 3 of 3 · The split shifts

Interest shrinks every month; principal grows

Month 1: about 29% of the $231.63 payment is interest. Month 60: under 1%.

Same total payment every month; only the split changes. Interest is charged on what is still owed, so as the balance falls, more of each payment reaches principal. At 7% for 60 months, principal exceeds interest from month 1 — on longer terms or higher rates, interest can be the larger share early on. The loan, modeled honestly
  • Down payment = MIN(price, savings); financed = price − down payment.
  • Loan monthly rate = contract rate ÷ 12. APR is not automatically that rate.
  • Payment = interest on the balance + principal; the split shifts every month.
  • 0% loans and fully saved cars still give sensible answers.
