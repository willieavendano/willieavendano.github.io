Predict · 1 min

Which loan is cheaper?

Same car, same down payment, same 7% rate

Loan A: 60 months. Loan B: 72 months, with a lower monthly payment. Which one costs less in total? Write one word and one reason.

“Cheaper” needs a unit: per month, or in total?

The trap is the word "cheaper." Both answers are defensible depending on whether it means the monthly payment or the total paid. The goal of the whole deck is that students always say which one they mean.

Sample values: 30 months of saving gives about $5,306.38, so about $10,693.62 is financed. Students build this table by copying their Loan sheet or recording outputs after each single change and then restoring the base. A row that changes two inputs at once cannot say which one caused the difference. Rate scenarios must be labeled as assumptions, not quotes.

Compare · 3 of 3

Three levers, three trade-offs

Term
longer term → lower payment, more total interest
Rate
higher rate → higher payment and more interest; there is no upside
Down payment
more saved → less borrowed → lower payment and less interest, but you wait longer

Your recommendation names which lever you would pull, and what it costs.

Push for the cost of each lever: the down-payment lever costs time (and risk that the car's price or the APY changes); the term lever costs interest; the rate lever is mostly outside the student's control in this model, so present it as a risk scenario.

Part Two

Owning Costs More Than Buying

The loan ends. Insurance, fuel and maintenance do not.

Ownership · 1 of 2

Estimate a monthly cost to own

CostHow to estimateFictional sample
Insurancea labeled classroom estimate or a published average with source$180/month
Fuel=miles_per_month/mpg*price_per_gallon; mpg from fueleconomy.gov600 ÷ 30 × $3.50 = $70/month
Maintenancea labeled estimate per month$50/month
Ownership total=SUM(...) of the three$300/month

Monthly cash need with the sample: $531.63 (60 months) or $499.43 (72 months).

Keep ownership costs in their own block, never inside the purchase price. The sample numbers are invented to be round and plausible; students must label theirs as estimates with a source or an explicit classroom assumption. No personal insurance quotes — quotes require personal information. fueleconomy.gov is the official DOE/EPA site for a model's combined mpg.

Never add a 60-month total to a 72-month total.

The same down payment is included in both columns so total cash out includes the purchase-day payment. The 60-month loan has no payment in months 61–72, which is why we compare over 72 months for both. The difference is still exactly the $461.55 of extra interest; ownership costs do not change which loan is cheaper in total, but they do change whether either payment is affordable.

Honest Limits

What this model cannot tell you

Affordability
income is not in the model, so it cannot say a payment is affordable
Real lending
real loans may accrue interest daily, add fees, or depend on credit
Moving prices
car prices, fees and APYs change while you save
Emergencies
spending all savings on the car leaves no reserve

Name at least one limit and what would change your decision.

This is the Communication criterion: "states the conclusion, the assumptions behind it, and what would change it." A recommendation with no stated limit tops out at Proficient. Unit 3 (Personal Budget) brings income in; this is the natural bridge.

Presentation · 2 of 2 · The write-up

A recommendation in 150–200 words

Sentence frame
  1. Saving $__ for __ months reaches $__; $__ of that is interest.
  2. That leaves $__ to finance at __%.
  3. I would choose __ months: $__/month and $__ total interest, versus …
  4. With ownership costs, my monthly need is about $__.
  5. This would change if __.

Every blank is a cell in your workbook.

The working sample page has a full model recommendation for the sample car; students may read it for structure but must write their own with their own numbers. Check that every number in the paragraph can be pointed to in a cell — that is the Formula Honesty standard applied to writing.

Rubric

What graders will test

Model Correctness
sample matches; zero-rate and fully saved cases behave sensibly
Formula Honesty

Savings and Loan sheets; inputs, schedule and outputs in separate blocks

Optional stretch: the Python script matches your payment to the cent.

These four criteria plus the optional Stretch are the kit's cornerstone rubric. The exemplar workbook (csm-u2-exemplars.xlsx) shows the same loan at each level; the What Changed tab is the fastest way for a student to see why a typed-in number drops them a level.

Peer Audit · 15 min

Ready or Revise?

Swap laptops. Mark each line Ready or Revise.
  • Research: car and two accounts have URLs, dates, fees, conditions.
  • Math: APY root for savings, rate ÷ 12 for the loan; zero-rate test passes.
  • Structure: change Savings!B4 — does the loan principal update?
  • Explanation: charts have units; scenarios hold other inputs constant.

Write one specific fix for every Revise.

This mirrors the HYSA checkpoint rubric on the kit page. Insist on a specific fix ("B7 has 0.07 typed in; point it at B5") instead of "check your formulas." Collect the audit sheets; they tell you who needs Tuesday conference time.
