Retrieve · 5 min

Your car research becomes four inputs

Listed price
from the listing you saved: year, make, model, mileage, URL, date
Taxes & fees
from the listing, or a labeled classroom assumption if it is not shown
Out-the-door price
a formula: =price + taxes_fees, never typed twice
Down-payment target
a dollar amount or a percent of out-the-door price

Sample: $15,000 car + $1,000 fees → $16,000 out the door.

Common failure: students treat MSRP or the sticker price as the cost of the car. Tax, title, registration and dealer fees are real; if the listing omits them, the student writes an assumption and labels it. Insurance, fuel and maintenance are monthly costs — keep them out of the purchase price; they return in Deck III. Students without saved research: give them the fictional $15,000 + $1,000 sample and move on.

Part One

Reading a Savings Account

The advertised rate is one line of the disclosure, not the whole account.

Research Record · 1 of 2

Record six things for each account

Who + where + when
institution, account name, product-page URL, the date you looked
APY
the number, and whether it is variable, fixed or promotional
Minimums
to open the account, and to earn the advertised rate
Fees
monthly fee and how to waive it
Insurance + eligibility
FDIC (banks) or NCUA (credit unions) statement; age rules
Access + one trade-off
transfer limits, how fast money moves, one downside

Missing from the disclosure? Write “not found” — never guess.

Use the institution's own product page and disclosure, not a ranking site. Rates change often; the observation date is what makes an old number honest. Students under 18 may not be eligible to open an account alone — that is a finding, not a dead end. No personal or family financial information is collected; this is a model.

The Rate Lesson · 1 of 3

APY already includes compounding

TermWhat it meansMonthly rate to use
APY — annual percentage yieldone year's effective growth on a balance, compounding included=(1+APY)^(1/12)-1
Nominal annual rate, compounded monthlya stated yearly rate split into 12 equal monthly rates=rate/12

Savings accounts advertise APY. Loans quote an annual interest rate. Different inputs, different conversions.

Definition: CFPB, Regulation DD §1030 Appendix A (annual percentage yield calculation).

This is the conceptual core of the deck. APY answers "how much does $1 become in one year?" so the monthly rate must be the 12th root of that growth, not a twelfth of the percent. The /12 conversion is correct for a nominal rate, which is what a loan contract states — students will use it Thursday. Watch for students who apply /12 to everything.

The Rate Lesson · 2 of 3

Turn an APY into a monthly rate

\( r = (1 + a)^{1/12} - 1 \)

StepSample: APY = 4.5%
Growth factor for a year\( 1 + 0.045 = 1.045 \)
12th root: one month's factor\( 1.045^{1/12} \approx 1.0036748 \)
Monthly rate\( r \approx 0.0036748 \approx 0.367\% \) per month
Check: 12 months back to a year\( 1.0036748^{12} \approx 1.045 \) ✓

Enter 4.5% as 4.5% or 0.045 — typing 4.5 means 450%.

The check row is the habit to build: undo the conversion and confirm you land on the APY. The 4.5-vs-4.5% trap is real and spectacular: with 4.5 typed, the 24-month balance is about $43,868 instead of $4,302. Any balance that big from $150 a month should trigger a reasonableness check.

The Rate Lesson · 3 of 3

Small errors are still errors

Wrong conversion
using 4.5% ÷ 12 as the monthly rate on an APY

  • Correct, 24 months: $4,302.33
  • APY ÷ 12: about $4,306.60
  • Overstates interest by about $4.27

Compounding frequency
hold a nominal 4.5% fixed, change how often it compounds

  • Monthly: APY ≈ 4.594%
  • Daily: APY ≈ 4.602%
  • Same one-year horizon for both

A fixed APY already absorbed the frequency question.

Left column: the error is small here because the rate and horizon are small — the point is that the model is wrong for a reason the student can name. Right column replaces the old practice-workbook drill (which compared 60 months to 60 days and converted APY by /12 — both errors). If a student asks "does daily compounding earn more?", the honest answer is: at the same nominal rate, slightly; at the same APY, no — the APY is the comparison number by design.

Predict · Turn and talk · 2 min

Deposits or interest?

Before you calculate

You start with $500, deposit $150 at the end of every month, and earn 4.5% APY. After 24 months, will most of the money come from your deposits or from interest? Write your guess and one reason.

Keep the guess. Tuesday's schedule will grade it.

Most students overestimate interest because "high-yield" sounds big. The schedule will show $4,100 of contributions and about $202 of interest. That surprise is the lesson for the recommendation: on a short horizon, the deposit amount matters far more than the rate. Collect a quick show of hands.

Build · 2 of 3 · Schedule

Two rows of formulas, copied to month 60

RowA MonthB OpeningC InterestD DepositE ClosingF Contributed

  • Headers go in row 10. Copy row 12 down through row 71 (month 60).
  • B8 ending balance: =INDEX(E11:E71,B5+1)
  • B9 interest earned: =B8-INDEX(F11:F71,B5+1)
Why INDEX with B5+1: month 0 sits in the first row of the range, so month m is position m+1. The table always runs to 60; B5 picks which month to report, so changing B5 never breaks the schedule. Watch for missing $ signs on B6 and B4 — rows below 12 then read empty cells and interest quietly becomes 0.

The zero-rate test

Set APY to 0%. The ending balance must equal starting savings plus deposits: \( 500 + 24 \times 150 = 4100 \). Then restore 4.5%.

These are the published checks from the activity document and the working sample page, so they are fine to show. The zero-rate test catches formulas that create money from nowhere (for example, adding the deposit twice). Answer the Monday prediction now: deposits $4,100, interest about $202.

Tuesday Task · 25 + 15 min

Chart it, then change one thing

Chart · 25 min

  • Title states the finding, not the topic
What-if · 15 min
  1. First month the balance reaches your target
  2. APY 2.0%, deposit fixed: new balance?
  3. Restore 4.5%; deposit $175: new balance?
  4. Which change mattered more?

One change at a time. Record what you held constant.

Teacher-only answers for the sample inputs (do not project): the $4,000 target is first reached in month 23 (about $4,137.13; month 22 is about $3,972.53). APY 2.0% gives about $4,189.41 at month 24; deposit $175 at 4.5% gives about $4,928.38. Over two years the deposit change matters far more. Gap between the two lines on the chart is the interest — ask students to point to it.

Screenshot this

Saving, modeled honestly
