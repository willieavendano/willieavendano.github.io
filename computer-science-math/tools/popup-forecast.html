    Formula Reference
    Revenue
    Revenue = Price × Units Sold
    $85 × 70 units = $5,950
    The total money you collect before subtracting any costs.
    Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
    COGS = Sum of all cost components per unit
    Ceramic blank $15 + Glaze $5 + Packaging $3 + Artisan fee $5 = $28
    Every direct cost that goes INTO making or sourcing one unit of your product.
    Gross Profit
    Gross Profit = Revenue − Total COGS
    $5,950 − (70 × $28) = $5,950 − $1,960 = $3,990
    Money left after paying for product costs, before rent, staff, and overhead.
    Gross Margin %
    Gross Margin % = (Gross Profit ÷ Revenue) × 100
    ($3,990 ÷ $5,950) × 100 = 67.1%
    The percentage of each dollar of revenue that remains after product costs. Higher is better.
    Contribution Margin
    Contribution Margin = Selling Price − Variable Cost per Unit
    $85 − $28 = $57 per planter
    How much each unit sold "contributes" toward covering fixed costs and profit.
    Break-Even Units
    Break-Even Units = Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution Margin per Unit
    $3,745 ÷ $57 = 66 planters/month
    The minimum number of units you must sell each month just to pay all your fixed costs.
    Break-Even Revenue
    Break-Even Revenue = Break-Even Units × Selling Price
    66 units × $85 = $5,610
    The minimum dollar amount of sales needed to cover all costs in a month.
    Net Profit
    Net Profit = Gross Profit − Fixed Costs
    $3,990 − $3,745 = $245
    The "bottom line" — what's left after EVERY cost is paid. Can be positive (profit) or negative (loss).
    Net Margin %
    Net Margin % = (Net Profit ÷ Revenue) × 100
    ($245 ÷ $5,950) × 100 = 4.1%
    The percentage of revenue that becomes actual profit after all costs. Retail businesses typically aim for 5–10%.
    Variance ($)
    Variance $ = Actual − Projected
    $6,200 actual − $5,950 projected = +$250 favorable
    How far off your forecast was in dollar terms. Positive = better than expected. Negative = worse.
    Variance (%)
    Variance % = Variance $ ÷ |Projected| × 100
    $250 ÷ $5,950 × 100 = +4.2%
    Variance as a percentage of the original projection. Helps compare across different-sized numbers.
    Business Vocabulary Glossary
    TermDefinition
    RevenueTotal money collected from sales before any deductions
    COGS (Cost of Goods Sold)Direct costs tied to producing each unit sold — materials, labor, packaging
    Gross ProfitRevenue minus COGS; profit before overhead costs
    Gross Margin %Gross profit as a percentage of revenue; measures product profitability
    Fixed CostsCosts that don't change with sales volume — rent, salaries, insurance
    Variable CostsCosts that increase as you sell more — materials, transaction fees, packaging
    Contribution MarginPrice minus variable costs per unit; what each sale contributes to covering fixed costs
    Break-Even PointThe sales level at which total revenue equals total costs — no profit, no loss
    Net ProfitRevenue minus ALL costs (COGS + fixed + other); the "bottom line"
    Net Margin %Net profit as a percentage of revenue; overall profitability ratio
    P&L StatementProfit & Loss statement — summarizes revenues and costs over a period
    Cash FlowActual cash moving in and out of the business over time
    Scenario AnalysisModeling different outcomes (conservative/realistic/optimistic) to understand risk
    OverheadIndirect operating expenses — rent, utilities, admin costs
    MarkupAmount added to cost to set a selling price (e.g., 200% markup on $28 COGS = $56 added → $84 price)
    Margin vs. MarkupMargin is profit ÷ price; markup is profit ÷ cost. They're different!
    Unit EconomicsPer-unit revenue, costs, and profit — the building blocks of business models
    Burn RateHow fast you're spending cash; important if you're losing money each month
    RunwayHow many months you can operate before running out of cash
    AmortizationSpreading a large one-time cost over multiple months (e.g., $2,100 fixtures ÷ 3 months = $700/month)
    VarianceThe difference between projected (forecast) and actual (real) results
    ForecastAn estimate of future financial performance based on assumptions
    CapitalMoney or assets invested in a business to generate profit
