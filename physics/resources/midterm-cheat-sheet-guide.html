Midterm Cheat-Sheet Guide — Three Units, One Half Sheet

Physics & Honors Physics 2026–27 · draft it Dec 4–6 with the study guide · refine it in review week (Dec 7–10) · bring it to the midterm (Dec 14–18)

The midterm policy. Your cheat sheet is half of a full sheet of paper: half of an 8.5″ × 11″ sheet, so 5.5″ × 8.5″. Fold a sheet of printer paper in half and cut along the fold. Use one side only, handwritten by you. Equations, definitions, triggers, and reminders only — no worked problems. Nothing typed, printed, photocopied, taped on, or borrowed. Cheat sheets are checked at the door, and a sheet that's bigger than a half sheet or written on both sides stays at the door.

Why this sheet is different

A half sheet is about twice the room of the 4×6 card you've used on unit tests — but it has to carry three units, so it's still a compression test: you decide what you actually still need. The exam prints the equation bank on its cover (kinematics, components, ΣF = ma, friction, ramp parts), so don't spend space copying it. Spend it on when to use each equation and the traps that have cost you points.

The build ritual (about 45 minutes, Dec 4–6)

  1. Retrieve (10 min). Without notes, redo the Know It Cold list in the study guide. Every ↻ goes on a scrap-paper list.
  2. Audit (10 min). Go through your Skills Check, Unit 1 Test, Unit 2 Test, and the corrections layer of your study guide. Every lost point becomes one line: what you did → what to do instead.
  3. Merge (10 min). Lay your old 4×6 cards side by side. Cross out anything you now know cold. Star anything you still looked up while doing the study guide.
  4. Compress (10 min). Rewrite each starred line in as few words as you can still explain (drill below). Typing this draft is fine — the sheet itself is handwritten.
  5. Handwrite & test (5 min + the sheet test). Copy onto your half sheet using the zone layout, then run the sheet test at the end of this guide.

Budget the space

Give each unit its own zone, plus a strip at the bottom for your personal traps and the K → D → E → A reminder. Unit 2 gets the most room because it's the newest. If one unit is your weak spot, give it more — but every unit needs something, because the exam tests all three. The planning template on the next page prints at true size (print at 100%, “actual size”): lay your half sheet on it, or use it as your draft.

The normal force is only equal to the weight when nothing else pushes or pulls vertically and the surface is level.N ≠ mg if angled F or ramp → read FBD
Static friction is not automatically μsN; it only matches the applied force until it reaches its maximum.fs ≤ μsN: compare, then decide
In a pulley problem the tension is not equal to the hanging weight, because the hanging mass is accelerating.T < m₂g if m₂ speeds up ↓

Leave off: worked problems (banned), the printed equation bank, full sentences, anything you already know cold, anything you can't explain out loud.

What earns a spot — unit by unit

Unit 0 · Toolkit

  • Prefix ladder as powers of ten (k, c, m, μ, n) — and cubed units cube the factor
  • Chain template: value × (new/old) × …; km/h ↔ m/s both ways
  • SOH-CAH-TOA — and “angle from vertical → swap”
  • Adding vectors: components → add → Pythagorean theorem → tan−1 → sketch to check the quadrant
  • Distance ≠ displacement; dimensional check: every term, same units

Unit 1 · Kinematics

  • v–t slope = a (m/s²) · v–t area = Δx (m) · flat v–t ≠ stopped
  • Equation chooser: which variable is missing?
  • Hidden givens: dropped → vi = 0; top → vy = 0; stops → vf = 0
  • Up +, a = −9.8 the whole flight; two roots? keep t > 0
  • Projectile: separate x and y lists, share only t; ax = 0; landing speed = √(vx² + vy²)

Unit 2 · Forces

  • FBD steps: dot → touches + gravity → arrows → labels → only forces ON it
  • Three laws in a few words each; constant v → ΣF = 0
  • 3rd-law pairs act on two objects → never on one FBD (N and mg are not a pair)
  • N from the vertical balance: angled pull up → N < mg; ramp → N = mg cosθ
  • Static: up to μsN — compare first, then decide; kinetic once sliding
  • Systems: whole system for a (tension cancels) → one object for T
  • Friction lab: f vs N slope = μ; static line steeper

Honors — add these

  • Quadrant check after tan−1
  • Elevator: N = m(g + a) when speeding up going up
  • Ramp slides alone if tanθ > μs
  • Ramp + pulley: tug-of-war first; friction points opposite the motion

The sheet test (night before)

  1. Close every note except your half sheet. Pick one study-guide problem per unit you haven't redone: try #2 (Unit 0), #7 (Unit 1), and #12 (Unit 2), then one capstone (#13–15).
  2. For each, find your first useful cue within 15 seconds. Then solve with full K / D / E / A.
  3. Stalled? Add the missing cue. Never looked at a line? Cut it.
  4. Read every line out loud. If you can't explain it, it's a liability — fix it or remove it.
