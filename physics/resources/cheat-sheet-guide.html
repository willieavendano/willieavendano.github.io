How to Build a Cheat Sheet That Actually Helps

Physics 2026–27 · your first card: one side of one 4×6 index card · due at the Skills Check, Monday, September 14

The policy. One side of one 4×6 index card. Handwritten by you — nothing typed, printed, photocopied, or borrowed. Equations, definitions, triggers, and reminders only — no worked problems. The card comes with you to every Skills Check and test this year, and the rules get stricter as you earn them: some future checks will shrink the card. Building it well is the studying — here's how.

Why the card exists

A cheat sheet is not a place to store answers — it's a machine that forces your brain to do three things it hates: retrieve (recall what the unit covered), judge (decide what you actually struggle with), and compress (shrink each idea until it fits). By the time the card is done, most of what's on it you'll barely need — and that's the point. Students who copy a friend's card or print one out skip all three steps and bring a luggage cart of text they'll never find under time pressure.

The hybrid move: type your brain-dump, delete ruthlessly until it fits a 4×6 box on screen, then hand-write the survivors onto the card. The typed list is scaffolding; the hand-copying is one more pass of retrieval before the check.

What makes the cut

  1. What you keep forgetting. Go back through PS1–PS3 and the study guide: every miss, every correction, every "wait, which one divides?" gets priority. What you already know cold does not belong on the card — it's spending space you don't have.
  2. Triggers and cues, not sentences. "exponents first!" beats "when comparing two numbers written in scientific notation, begin by comparing their exponents." The card cues your memory; it doesn't replace it.
  3. Rules with the reason compressed out. "answer w/o unit = no credit" — five words carry a whole class period.
  4. Equations and conversion factors, units attached. A formula without its units is half a formula.
  5. Decoders and warnings. The Greek alphabet, the DEG-mode check, the ≈-versus-= rule — the small stuff that costs big points.

Leave off: worked problems (banned, and useless — you'd need a new one anyway), definitions you can re-derive in one line, full sentences, anything you can't explain out loud. If you can't explain a line on your own card, that line is a liability — know every line you bring.

The build ritual

  1. Brain-dump (10 min). On scrap paper, list everything Unit 0 covered, fast, no filter. Slower than the memory, the page — write until it stops coming.
  2. Triage. Cross off what you know cold. Star what you got wrong or looked up on PS1–PS3 and the study guide. The stars write first.
  3. Compress. Shrink each survivor to a cue of five words or fewer. Say it out loud as a full sentence to check it still triggers the whole idea.
  4. Zone the card in pencil. Title across the top, three columns, one strip along the bottom. Pencil first — one wasted card is fine; two is triage you skipped.
  5. Ink it. Biggest ideas biggest. Units in every formula. Nothing crammed at the bottom edge.
  6. Card test (below). Then adjust — space you never touched is space someone else deserved.

Unit 0: things to know (checklist)

Everything below is fair game Monday. Check items as you place them — or as you consciously decide they're already automatic.

  • SI base units and symbols: mass kg, time s, length m — and which common unit is derived (N = kg·m/s²)
  • Prefix powers: G 10⁹ · M 10⁶ · k 10³ · c 10⁻² · m 10⁻³ · μ 10⁻⁶ · n 10⁻⁹, and which way the number moves in each conversion
  • Scientific notation: coefficient 1–10 × 10n, converting both directions
  • Comparing powers of ten without writing them out
  • Sig figs: leading zeros never, trapped zeros always, trailing only with a decimal, 1200-style ambiguity
  • KDEA, labeled, on every quantitative problem
  • Units travel with the math; an answer without a unit does not count
  • Symbols rearranged before numbers go in
  • ≈ for rounded, = only when exact
  • SOH-CAH-TOA — and how to pick the ratio (side you know + side you want)
  • Opposite/adjacent flip when you switch angles; hypotenuse never moves
  • Inverse trig for angles: θ = tan−1(opp/adj)
  • Pythagorean theorem, both directions: unknown hypotenuse and unknown leg
  • Components: x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ
  • A vector is magnitude AND direction — both halves or half credit
  • The 3-4-5 family and its ≈37°/53° angles
  • The Greek decoder: Δ Σ θ ω λ μ ρ τ
  • Calculator in DEG mode — the sin 30° = 0.5 check
  • Fermi estimation: 3–4 guessables, written down, multiplied to an order of magnitude

The card test

  1. Sit down with the finished card and work Study Guide problems 7 and 11 fresh — no other notes.
  2. Every time you reach for the card, tally it. Anything you looked for and didn't find goes on before Monday.
  3. Anything you never touched is wasting space — erase it and give that real estate to a starred item.
  4. Last check: read the card top to bottom and explain every line out loud in one sentence. A line you can't explain is a line you can't use.

Bring the card Monday, September 14 — handwritten, one side, 4×6. It rides with you the rest of the year.
