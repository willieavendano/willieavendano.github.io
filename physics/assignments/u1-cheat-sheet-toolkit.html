Unit 1 Cheat-Sheet Toolkit

Build a card that helps you choose your next step. The building is part of the studying.

Use the class card rules: one side of one 4 × 6 index card, handwritten by you. Equations, definitions, triggers, and reminders only; no worked problems. Follow any updated exam-specific directions from Mr. Avendano. This one-pager is a planning tool.

1 / Choose what earns space

  1. Recall first. List the Unit 1 ideas you remember without notes.
  2. Check your corrections. Star repeated mistakes on PS4-6 and the review.
  3. Compress. Turn each mistake into a short cue you can explain.
  4. Draft, then handwrite. Use clear headings and readable symbols.

Useful cue: “Same time; separate x/y.”
Skip: paragraphs, copied solutions, facts you know cold.

2 / Give the card four zones

GRAPH RULES
Slope, area, signs, units
EQUATION CHOOSER
Formula + when it helps
FREE FALL / PROJECTILES
Axes, components, shared time
MY ERROR CHECKS
Your most useful reminders

Add a narrow bottom strip: K → D → E → A.
Givens + units → target → equation → rearrange, substitute, check.

Put your biggest trouble spot first. Avoid tiny writing.

3 / Add cues, not just formulas

Constant acceleration in one axis: choose the equation that leaves out the variable you do not have or need.

EquationLeaves out
vf = vi + atΔx
Δx = vit + ½at²vf
vf² = vi² + 2aΔxt
Δx = ½(vi + vf)ta

Vertical motion: use Δy.
Δx = xf − xi; velocity m/s; acceleration m/s²; time s.

Honors: add model conditions and a physical-root reminder.

4 / Keep the graph rules visible

  • x-t slope = velocity. Horizontal line means at rest.
  • v-t slope = acceleration. Horizontal line means constant velocity.
  • Signed v-t area = displacement. Total distance adds the absolute areas.
  • a-t signed area = Δv.
  • Velocity and acceleration with the same sign: speeding up. Opposite signs: slowing down.

Write your sign convention

“Negative velocity” means motion in your negative direction. It does not automatically mean slowing down.

Check slope/area units against your interpretation.

5 / Build a projectile checklist

  1. Sketch axes and choose a positive direction.
  2. If θ is above horizontal:
    vix = vi cos θ; viy = vi sin θ.
  3. Ignore air resistance: ax = 0. With up positive, ay = −9.8 m/s².
  4. Use vertical motion to find the time for the target event; then use Δx = vixt.

Keep x/y KDEA lists separate. Both axes share time, not velocity. At the peak vy = 0, but gravity still acts.

6 / Save space for error traps

  • Dropped: viy = 0. Horizontal launch: viy = 0; vix may be nonzero.
  • At the peak: only vertical velocity is zero; horizontal velocity can remain nonzero.
  • Same launch/landing height? Δy = 0. Otherwise, use the actual height change.
  • For angles in degrees, check DEG mode.
  • Rearrange before substituting. Check units, direction, and whether the result fits the story.
Five-minute card test: Close your notes. Pick one fresh review problem. Find your first useful cue within 15 seconds, then solve with KDEA. If you stall, add the missing cue; remove a line you never use. Explain every line before you bring the card.
