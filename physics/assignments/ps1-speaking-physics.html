How to work this set. Show your reasoning, not just answers — on conversions, write the power of ten you used; on estimates, write every guess you multiplied. 1Name that unitSI units

For each quantity, give the SI unit we will measure it in and its symbol. Then circle the one quantity below whose unit is derived (built from base units) rather than a base unit itself.

  1. The mass of a lab cart
  2. The time for the cart to cross the track
  3. The length of the track
  4. The force your hand pushes the cart with
2Prefixes: back to baseSI units

Convert each measurement to its SI base unit. For each one, follow the four steps from class: identify the prefix, write its power of ten, multiply through, and sanity-check the direction (should the number get bigger or smaller?).

  1. A 7.2 km morning run, in meters
  2. A 350 ms human reaction time, in seconds
  3. A 2.4 MW wind turbine, in watts
  4. Green light with a wavelength of 530 nm, in meters (scientific notation is fine here)
Work
3Units in a trench coatSI units

A classmate solves a force problem and proudly reports the answer F = 12 m/s.

  1. Without checking a single line of their arithmetic, how do you already know the answer is wrong?
  2. Write the newton in terms of SI base units, and say what quantity does get measured in m/s.
4Counting significant figuresMeasurement

How many significant figures are in each measurement? For (e), explain in one sentence why the count is ambiguous.

  1. 0.00470 kg
  2. 302 m
  3. 5.60 × 10³ s
  4. 0.5080 L
  5. 1200 m
5Read it out loudVariable notation

Write out each expression in plain English, the way you would read it to the class. Remember: below the line is a name tag, above the line is math.

  1. vi
  2. x0
  3. vf2
  4. Fnet

e. μs and μk are the same Greek letter wearing two different subscripts. What is the physical difference between them?

7Scientific notationNotation
  1. Write 92,900,000 m (a satellite's distance) in scientific notation.
  2. Write 0.0000088 kg (a grain of sand) in scientific notation.
  3. Write 6.1 × 10−4 s as an ordinary decimal number.
  4. Which is larger, 3 × 104 or 8 × 10³? Explain how you can tell without writing either number out in full.
8Fermi problem: the pizza censusEstimation

Estimate: how many slices of pizza do the students of one Miami high school eat in a school year? Do not look anything up — that's the point.

  1. Break the question into three or four smaller quantities you can reasonably guess, and write down your guess for each one.
  2. Multiply them together and report your result as an order of magnitude (thousands? tens of thousands? hundreds of thousands?).
  3. Which of your guesses are you least confident in? If it were off by a factor of 2, would your order of magnitude change?
Work

Graded on the homework 4–3–2–1 scale — completeness and honest reasoning, not just right answers. There is no single correct number for Problem 8; there are only defensible ones.
