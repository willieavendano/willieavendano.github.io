How to work this version. Same seven problems, same skills, same selection rule — find the one variable you neither know nor want, and use the equation that omits it — but the numbers are rougher and most problems add one extra move. Watch the units: some givens arrive in km/h, and one answer is a speed you have to convert back. Name the missing variable in your E row every time. Symbols first, numbers last, units the whole way.
1Pick the equation — don't solveSelection rule

For each scenario, name the missing variable (neither known nor wanted) and write the equation you'd use. No arithmetic. Two of these hide the missing variable behind a unit conversion or an unusual target — read carefully.

  1. A jet starts from rest and accelerates at 3.5 m/s² down 900 m of runway. You want its takeoff speed.
  2. A train slows from 144 km/h to 36 km/h over 60 s. You want how far it traveled. (Convert first.)
  3. A dropped phone falls 1.6 m. You want its speed just before impact. (Note: you are not given the time.)
  4. A sprinter accelerates steadily at 2.5 m/s² from 3.0 m/s to 9.0 m/s. You want the distance covered while speeding up.

5Three-stage tripMulti-step

A motorcycle starts from rest, accelerates at 4.0 m/s² for 6.0 s, then holds a constant velocity for another 8.0 s, then brakes uniformly to rest in 5.0 s.

  1. How fast is it moving at the end of the first stage?
  2. How far does it travel during the first stage?
  3. How far during the constant-velocity stage? (Careful — the four-equation bank isn't even needed here.)
  4. What is its acceleration during braking, sign included?
  5. Total distance for the whole trip?
Work
6Speeding up, slowing down, or reversing?Signs & motion

For each part, use the sign rule (same signs vs. opposite signs), and compute a value where one is asked for.

  1. v = +14 m/s, a = −3 m/s². Speeding up or slowing down?
  2. An object moving at −6 m/s speeds up to −9 m/s in 1.5 s. Find its acceleration, then state the motion.
  3. v = +5 m/s, a = +5 m/s². Speeding up or slowing down?
  4. An object is moving at +8 m/s with a = −8 m/s². Describe its motion over the next 1.0 s — including what happens at t = 1.0 s and after.
  5. One sentence: why does "negative acceleration" alone tell you nothing about whether the object is slowing down?
Work
7Grade this work — two errorsCritique

The problem: "A car moving at 30 m/s brakes uniformly to rest in 6.0 s. How far does it travel while stopping?" A student writes:

Δx = vf t + ½ a t² = (0)(6.0) + ½(−5.0)(36) = −90 m

  1. The value a = −5.0 m/s² is correct. Find both things wrong with the student's final line — one is a subscript, one is about what a distance can and cannot be.
  2. Fix it: run the full KDEA and report the real stopping distance. Sanity-check the sign of your answer.
