Why this matters. Next week the cart lab asks you to plot position x against t2 — not against t. Before you can trust that move, you have to be able to look at an equation and decide, without guessing, which two variables will make a straight line. That decision is linearization, and it is the single skill behind every graph you will fit this year: carts, friction, energy, collisions, pendulums.

The vocabulary of proportionality

“Is proportional to”

A variable y is proportional to x (written y ∝ x) when one is a constant multiple of the other: y = kx, with k a constant. The test is simple: if you divide one by the other, the result never changes.

The exponent on a variable tells you how to plot it. The trick is to move every variable into the first power before you graph.

EquationProportionalityPlot that gives a straight lineCurved if you plot…
y = kxy ∝ xy vs x—
y = kx2y ∝ x2y vs x2y vs x
y = k/xy ∝ 1/xy vs 1/xy vs x
y2 = kxy ∝ √xy2 vs xy vs x

One rule, four shapes: a straight line appears when each side of the equation holds a variable to the first power.
