New Concept

The Doppler Effect

Motion changes the frequency an observer hears.

Open with a siren or train whistle if available, but do not let the demonstration become the explanation. The essential question is: what changes for the observer, and what stays the same for the source? Students should leave this divider ready to separate source frequency from observed frequency.

Doppler · 1 of 4

Pitch shifts because wavefronts bunch up or spread out.

Toward
source and observer approach
Wavefronts reach the observer more often → observed frequency increases → pitch sounds higher.
Away
source and observer recede
Wavefronts reach the observer less often → observed frequency decreases → pitch sounds lower.
Still
no closing motion
The observer hears the source frequency: no Doppler shift from relative motion.

Approach means higher pitch. Recede means lower pitch. Make the prediction first.

Use the word "observed" repeatedly. A source can emit a constant frequency while the observer hears a changing frequency. The most common failure mode is reversing higher and lower; have students make the hand motion of compressed versus spread wavefronts before discussing equations.

Doppler · 2 of 4

Ask two questions before touching a formula.

Reasoning protocol
  1. Are the source and observer moving toward each other or away from each other?
  2. Should the observer receive wavefronts more often or less often?
  3. Therefore, is the observed pitch higher or lower?
Keep constant

For a stationary medium, the wave speed in air is set by the medium and temperature.

Change for the listener

The arrival rate of wavefronts changes, so the observed frequency changes.

This slide is intentionally qualitative. If students rush to signs, stop them and make them complete the three questions in words. The second common failure mode is claiming the wave speed changes because the source moves; in the classroom model, source motion changes spacing and arrival rate, not the speed of sound through the medium.

Doppler · 3 of 4

The algebra agrees with the prediction.

Source moving, observer stationary

fobs = fs · v / (v ± vs)

  • fobs — observed frequency in Hz
  • fs — source frequency in Hz
  • v — wave speed in the medium in m/s
  • vs — source speed in m/s

Approaching source: subtract in the denominator. Receding source: add in the denominator.

Keep this equation scoped to the source-moving, observer-stationary model. Do not introduce a larger sign convention than students need for this course. The denominator rule has a conceptual reason: approach compresses the wavelength and increases the arrival rate; receding motion stretches the wavelength and decreases it.

Doppler · 4 of 4 · KDEA

A source approaches: predict, then calculate.

A siren emits fs = 700 Hz while moving toward a stationary listener at vs = 20 m/s. Take the speed of sound as v = 343 m/s. Find the observed frequency.

StepWhat you write
Kfs = 700 Hz, v = 343 m/s, vs = 20 m/s; source approaches
Dobserved frequency fobs, target unit Hz
Efobs = fs · v / (v − vs) for approach
Afobs = (700 Hz)(343 m/s) / (343 m/s − 20 m/s) ≈ 743 Hz

743 Hz > 700 Hz: the higher pitch matches the approach prediction.

Model the prediction before the substitution: the listener should hear a higher pitch, so the final frequency must be greater than 700 Hz. In the A row, point out that the m/s units cancel and Hz remains. The numerical value is rounded, so use ≈, not an equals sign.

Doppler Practice · Turn & Talk · 8 minutes

Make the prediction without calculating.

For each scenario, state higher / lower / unchanged and justify with wavefront spacing.
  1. A motorcycle moves toward you while its engine emits a steady tone.
  2. You move toward a stationary speaker playing one note.
  3. A train moves away from you after passing the platform.
  4. You and a source move side by side at the same speed and direction.

Your justification must mention relative motion, not just the word “Doppler.”

Listen for the transfer that observer motion also matters. Students often memorize only "moving source toward me means higher" and then fail the speaker example. The side-by-side case is a useful boundary condition: if there is no closing motion along the line between source and observer, there is no shift in this simplified model.

Unit 8 Review

One wave model. Five lenses.

Properties · energy · interference · standing waves · sound

The review begins by organizing the unit, not by displaying a formula dump. Keep the five lenses visible as students work: describe the wave, track energy, combine waves, identify resonance, connect the model to sound.

Review · 1 of 5

Wave properties: name the pattern before the math.

A
amplitude · m
Maximum displacement from equilibrium. It tracks energy carried by the wave, not how fast the pattern repeats.
λ
wavelength · m
Distance between matching points in phase: crest to crest or compression to compression.
f
frequency · Hz
Cycles per second. 1 Hz = 1/s. For sound, frequency is heard as pitch.

Wave speed is the pattern speed: v = f · λ, with v in m/s.

The common misconception is that amplitude and frequency are interchangeable descriptions of "bigger" waves. Amplitude is displacement and energy; frequency is repetition rate and pitch. Ask students to point to the visual feature that represents each quantity.

Review · Wave Equation · KDEA

A wavelength and frequency set the speed.

A wave has wavelength λ = 0.80 m and frequency f = 12 Hz. Find its speed.

StepWhat you write
Kλ = 0.80 m, f = 12 Hz
Dwave speed v, target unit m/s
Ev = f · λ
Av = (12 cycles/s)(0.80 m/cycle) ≈ 9.6 m/s

The cycle label cancels, leaving meters per second.

This is a units-first review. Students may write Hz as if it were an unrelated symbol; rewrite 12 Hz as 12 cycles/s and show the cycle cancellation. The equation is an identity, so equals signs are appropriate there; the rounded result uses ≈.

Review · 2 of 5

Superposition: the medium adds the displacements.

+
Constructive interference
Displacements reinforce. Crest + crest gives a larger crest; trough + trough gives a deeper trough.
−
Destructive interference
Displacements oppose. Equal and opposite disturbances can produce zero displacement at one instant.
The rule

ytotal = y1 + y2

Interference changes the temporary shape of the medium. It does not mean either wave disappears or loses its energy forever.

Ask: are the displacements in the same direction at the point you care about?

Students often say destructive interference destroys energy. Correct that language: the net displacement can be zero while energy continues through the system. Keep the question local—interference depends on the displacements at the same place and same time.

Review · 3 of 5

Standing waves are interference patterns that stay put.

Node
always zero displacement
A point of complete destructive interference. Adjacent loops move on opposite sides of the node.
Antinode
maximum amplitude
A point of constructive interference. The largest oscillation occurs between neighboring nodes.
L
fixed end · string
A node occurs at each fixed end. Allowed patterns fit half-wavelength segments into the length.
Resonance
natural frequency match
Driving frequency matches an allowed natural mode, producing a large-amplitude response.

Standing-wave drawings are evidence: count nodes, antinodes, and fitted λ segments.

The common failure mode is labeling every peak as an antinode without checking the boundary condition. Have students trace the string: fixed ends are nodes, and each loop is one half-wavelength. Resonance is not "any big vibration"; it is a frequency match to an allowed mode.

Review · 4 of 5

Sound is a mechanical longitudinal wave.

C
compressions
Particles are closer together; pressure is higher than equilibrium.
R
rarefactions
Particles are farther apart; pressure is lower than equilibrium.
Air
medium required
Sound cannot travel through a vacuum. The medium oscillates parallel to the direction of travel.

Amplitude relates to loudness; frequency relates to pitch; waveform relates to timbre.

Separate the physical wave from the perception vocabulary. Loudness is not frequency, and pitch is not amplitude. Students also frequently draw sound as a transverse wave because that is the easiest picture; ask them to show particle motion parallel to propagation and label compressions and rarefactions.

Review · 5 of 5

Sound resonates when the air column supports a mode.

Reason from boundaries
  1. Identify the open-end and closed-end behavior for the air column.
  2. Mark pressure nodes and antinodes, or displacement nodes and antinodes.
  3. Fit the allowed wavelength pattern into the tube length.
  4. Use v = f · λ only after the standing-wave pattern is clear.
Open end

Pressure tends toward atmospheric pressure: pressure node.

Closed end

Air displacement is restricted: displacement node.

Boundary-language errors are the main trap here. Students may switch pressure and displacement labels mid-problem. Choose one representation and stay consistent; if using pressure, open is a node and closed is an antinode. If using displacement, the labels reverse.

Unit 8 · Concept Map

Connect the unit with one chain of questions.

1
Describe
What are A, λ, f, T, and v? Which units belong to each quantity?
2
Combine
When waves overlap, do their displacements add or oppose at the point of interest?
3
Constrain
What boundary conditions decide the nodes, antinodes, and allowed standing-wave modes?
4
Interpret
For sound, what does the listener perceive: loudness, pitch, or timbre? Is there relative motion?

KDEA turns each question into organized physics instead of a formula hunt.

This is the review anchor. Students should be able to place any test question into at least one tile, often two. The most important transfer is from a visual or verbal description to a quantity with units before they calculate.

Common Test Traps

Four ways to lose a wave problem.

1
frequency vs. period
They are reciprocals: f = 1/T. Do not use seconds as if they were hertz.
2
amplitude vs. wavelength
Vertical size is amplitude; horizontal repeat distance is wavelength.
3
Doppler direction
Approach → higher observed frequency. Recede → lower observed frequency.
4
boundary conditions
Count fitted half-wavelengths and label nodes consistently before using v = f · λ.

If the prediction and the arithmetic disagree, stop and inspect the model first.

These are not four random reminders; they are model-selection errors. Have students explain which representation they misread. A correct calculation from the wrong quantity is still wrong physics, so the prediction and unit check belong before the final answer.

Test Practice · Turn & Talk · 12 minutes

A wave reaches a listener.

KDEA on your whiteboard

A stationary speaker emits a tone at 500 Hz. A listener walks toward the speaker at 2.0 m/s. In words:

  1. Predict whether the observed pitch is higher, lower, or unchanged.
  2. Identify which relative motion matters.
  3. Choose the quantities and units you would need for a numerical model.
  4. Write a one-sentence explanation using wavefront arrival rate.

No answer key on the slide: show your reasoning, not just a choice.

This deliberately asks for a model without forcing the full observer-motion equation. The goal is to catch the misconception that only source motion creates a Doppler shift. Students should say the listener approaches the wavefronts, so the arrival rate increases and the pitch is higher.

The Test · 1 of 2

What the Unit 8 Test can ask you to do

1
Read wave data
Identify amplitude, wavelength, period, frequency, and wave speed with correct units.
2
Explain interference
Use superposition to predict constructive or destructive interference at a point.
3
Analyze resonance
Use nodes, antinodes, boundary conditions, and fitted λ segments to reason about standing waves.
4
Explain sound + Doppler
Connect pressure patterns, loudness, pitch, and relative motion to an observed sound.

A complete answer names the model, shows units, and explains what the result means.

Be direct about scope: this is a Unit 8 test, not a vocabulary spelling bee. A strong response combines a diagram, a relationship, and a physical interpretation. Students who only memorize v = fλ will be underprepared for interference, resonance, and Doppler reasoning.

Recap

Screenshot this before Thursday.

Unit 8 in five moves
  • Wave properties — v = f · λ; amplitude, wavelength, frequency, period: each its own meaning and unit.
  • Interference — displacements superpose: reinforce or oppose at the same place and time.
  • Standing waves — node/antinode patterns from interference plus boundary conditions.
  • Sound — mechanical longitudinal waves; amplitude → loudness, frequency → pitch.
  • Doppler — approach → higher observed frequency; recede → lower.
Give students a quiet minute to compare this recap with their own notes. The key test-prep move is retrieval: cover one bullet and reconstruct the definition, units, and one example from memory. Do not turn this into a last-minute lecture; let the gaps become visible. House line worth saying out loud as they leave: units travel with the math, explanations travel with the model.
