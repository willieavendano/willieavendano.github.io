The Protocol

KDEA: The Way to Solve ANY Problem in the World

K
Know
Every given, as a symbol with its unit. Pick a positive direction first.
D
Don't know
The target symbol and the unit it must come out in.
E
Equations
The equation that links K to D. For kinematics: the one without the missing variable.
A
Algebra
Symbols first, then numbers with units, then box the answer with its unit.

On the midterm, a bare final number earns zero credit, even when it's right.

Same wording as every unit test: say it exactly. The midterm is graded the same way the unit tests were. Before you go over the Unit 1 and 2 Tests, check where points were lost; expect most of it in K (missing units, no sign convention) and E (equation picked by habit rather than by the missing variable), not in A.

[ ]
Dimensional analysis
Units travel with the math. If the units come out wrong, the algebra is wrong.
△
SOH-CAH-TOA
Components: x = v·cos θ, y = v·sin θ, when θ is measured from the horizontal.
The component rule is the single most reused Unit 0 skill: projectiles (Unit 1), angled pulls and inclines (Unit 2). Stress "when θ is measured from the horizontal" — the sin/cos swap is the most common error, and it comes from an angle measured from the vertical.

Unit 0 · Quick Check · Pairs · 8 minutes

Three toolkit moves

With your partner, units on every line
  1. A car moves at 72 km/h. Convert to m/s using a conversion chain.
  2. Write 0.000 450 kg in scientific notation, then in grams.
  3. A ball leaves your foot at 15 m/s, 25° above the horizontal. Find both components.

Draw the triangle for #3 before you touch the calculator. Calculator in degree mode.

Answers: (1) 72 km/h × (1000 m / 1 km) × (1 h / 3600 s) = 20 m/s. (2) 4.50 × 10−4 kg = 0.450 g. (3) vx = 15 cos 25° ≈ 13.6 m/s, vy = 15 sin 25° ≈ 6.34 m/s. Check calculators are in degree mode — a radian-mode calculator gives vx ≈ 14.9 m/s and nobody notices.

Unit 1 · Tuesday

Kinematics

How things move: graphs, four equations, free fall, projectiles.

Unit 1 · Know These Cold

Six things from kinematics

x–t
Slope = velocity
Straight line: constant v. Curve: v is changing.
v–t
Slope = a, area = Δx
The area between the line and the time axis is the displacement.
4
Four equations
Each one leaves out one variable. Pick the one without your missing variable.
g
Free fall
a = −9.8 m/s2 (up positive) the whole flight, including at the top.
x | y
Projectiles
Horizontal: ax = 0. Vertical: free fall. They share only t.
x vs t2
The cart lab
x vs t2 is straight from rest, with slope ½a, so a = 2 × slope.
The cart-lab tile is fair game: linearization was the analysis in Lab Report I and in the ball drop. Expect students to be able to say why x vs t² is straight and how to get a from the slope.

Unit 1 · Worked KDEA

A marble rolls off a 1.25 m table at 3.0 m/s

StepVertical: how long in the air?Horizontal: how far?
Kviy = 0 m/s, Δy = −1.25 m, a = −9.8 m/s2vx = 3.0 m/s, ax = 0, t from the left
Dt, in sΔx, in m
EΔy = viyt + ½at2 — no vfyΔx = vxt
A\( t = \sqrt{2\Delta y / a} = \sqrt{2(-1.25)/(-9.8)} \approx 0.505\ \text{s} \)\( \Delta x = (3.0)(0.505) \approx 1.5\ \text{m} \)

Two passes, one shared t. The two negatives cancel because Δy and a point the same way.

Model the full table. viy = 0 because the launch is horizontal — the whole 3.0 m/s is vx. The answer is 1.5 m, not 1.52 m: the 3.0 m/s carries two significant figures. Ask: "what would change if the marble left at 6.0 m/s?" — t stays the same, Δx doubles. That is the independence idea in one line.

Unit 2 · Tuesday

Forces & Newton's Laws

Why things move: draw the forces, add them, divide by the mass.

Unit 2 · Know These Cold

Six things from forces

1st
Inertia
ΣF = 0 means constant velocity, which includes standing still.
ΣF = ma
Second law
Net force, not any one force. Write it along each axis separately.
3rd
Pairs
Equal and opposite, on two different objects. Never on the same FBD.
FBD
Free-body diagram
Only forces ON the object, each labeled with what exerts it.
μ
Friction
fk = μkN. Static friction can be anything up to μsN.
∠
Inclines & systems
Tilt the axes to the ramp. For connected objects, treat them as one first.
"Static friction can be anything up to μsN" is the line students most often get wrong: they write fs = μsN for a box that isn't moving. It equals μsN only at the moment the box is about to slip.

Unit 2 · The Normal Force

N isn't always mg

SituationPerpendicular force balanceNormal force
Box on flat groundN − mg = 0N = mg
Pulled at θ above horizontalN + F sin θ − mg = 0N = mg − F sin θ
Pushed down at θ below horizontalN − F sin θ − mg = 0N = mg + F sin θ
On a ramp at angle θN − mg cos θ = 0N = mg cos θ

Find N from the FBD every time. Friction is μ times that N, not μ times mg.

Quick oral check: a 10 kg box pulled with 50 N at 30° above horizontal. N = 98 − 50 sin 30° = 98 − 25 = 73 N, not 98 N. A friction answer built on 98 N is wrong by about a third.

Unit 2 · Worked KDEA

A block slides down a 30° ramp, μk = 0.20

StepWhat you write
Kθ = 30°, μk = 0.20, g = 9.8 m/s2; down the ramp is positive
Da, in m/s2
Einto the ramp: N = mg cos θ  ·  along the ramp: mg sin θ − μkN = ma
A\( a = g(\sin\theta - \mu_k\cos\theta) = 9.8(0.500 - 0.173) \approx 3.2\ \text{m/s}^2 \)

The mass cancels. Without friction, a would be g sin θ ≈ 4.9 m/s2.

Draw the FBD first: weight straight down, N perpendicular to the ramp, friction up the ramp (opposite the motion). Then tilt the axes and split the weight into mg sin θ along and mg cos θ into the ramp. The cancelled mass is worth a sentence: "a heavier block slides with the same acceleration." Answer has 2 sig figs because μ does.

Unit 2 · Connected Objects

Systems: one object first, then one piece

Step 1 · the whole system

Only forces from outside the system count. Tension inside the string cancels.

a = ΣFext / mtotal

Step 2 · one block alone

Now draw one block's FBD. Tension is an outside force on it. Use the a from step 1 to find T.

T = mblocka (frictionless table)

Try it: a 3.0 kg cart on a frictionless table is tied over a pulley to a hanging 2.0 kg mass. Find a and T.

Answers: a = (2.0)(9.8) / 5.0 ≈ 3.9 m/s²; T = (3.0)(3.92) ≈ 12 N. Check: the hanging mass alone, mg − T = ma → 19.6 − 11.8 ≈ 7.8 = (2.0)(3.9). The common error is T = mg = 19.6 N, which would mean the hanging mass doesn't accelerate.

Units 1 & 2 · The Labs

What the two lab reports taught

I
Cart lab
Straighten a curve: plot x vs t2, take the slope from two points on the line, a = 2 × slope, ± from the steepest and shallowest lines.
II
Friction lab
Plot friction against normal force: a straight line through the origin with slope μ. The static line is steeper than the kinetic one.

Expect a graph question: what to plot, why it is straight, and what the slope means.

Keep the claim general — "expect a graph question" — unless the midterm form is final. Both labs share the same move: choose axes so the model is a straight line, then read the physics off the slope.

The Traps · Units 0–2

Six ways to lose points you already earned

°
Radian mode
Check degree mode before the first trig key.
±
Sign of g
Up positive means a = −9.8 m/s2, even at the top.
x ≠ y
Mixed directions
No equation holds an x quantity and a y quantity. Only t is shared.
N
N = mg by habit
Angled pulls and ramps change N. Read it off the FBD.
3rd
Pairs on one FBD
A third-law pair acts on two objects, so it never cancels on one diagram.
m/s
No units
A number without a unit is not an answer.
Ask the room which of the six cost them points on the Unit 1 or Unit 2 Test, by show of hands. Have each student write the two that apply to them at the top of their practice page for the rest of the period.

Mixed Practice · Pairs · 25 minutes

One problem from each unit, full KDEA

Label K, D, E, A on every problem
  1. A car speeds up from 12 m/s to 28 m/s over 64 m. Find its acceleration.
  2. A stone is thrown straight up at 14.7 m/s. How high does it rise, and how long does that take?
  3. A 50 kg crate is pushed across the floor with a 220 N horizontal force; μk = 0.30. Find its acceleration.

Before E, say the missing variable out loud (1, 2) or draw the FBD (3).

Answers: (1) no t → vf² = vi² + 2aΔx; a = (28² − 12²) / (2·64) = 640 / 128 = 5.0 m/s². (2) at the top vf = 0; Δy = vi² / (2g) = 216.09 / 19.6 ≈ 11 m; t = 14.7 / 9.8 = 1.5 s. (3) N = mg = 490 N; fk = 0.30 · 490 = 147 N; ΣF = 220 − 147 = 73 N; a = 73 / 50 ≈ 1.5 m/s². Problem 2 is the place to catch the sign error: with up positive, a = −9.8 and Δy comes out positive. Then hand back the Unit 2 / Midterm Review worksheet (PS9) for the rest of the block.

Know Cold

Write these from memory before the exam

  • The prefixes k, c, m, μ as powers of ten
  • x = v·cos θ, y = v·sin θ, and when they swap
  • The slope chain: x–t → v, v–t → a, v–t area → Δx
  • The four kinematics equations and what each one leaves out
  • g = 9.8 m/s2 downward, the whole flight
  • Newton's three laws, each in one sentence
  • ΣF = ma, fk = μkN, fs ≤ μsN
  • On a ramp: mg sin θ along it, mg cos θ into it

If you can't write one from memory, that's your first study item.
